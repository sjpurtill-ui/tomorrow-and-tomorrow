# Geoscience subject art

Both images use apprentice_contracts.png as the style reference and were generated separately with the built-in image tool. Both were visually inspected before installation. The stratigraphy scene makes continuous rock layers the main subject, with two small observers. The mineral scene shows a lone worker making red and black powder streaks on a light tile. Both preserve square warm ivory paper, subdued gouache, low subjects and substantial negative space. No text, symbols or modern equipment appear.

## Relative Stratigraphy

Create one new square research illustration, Relative Stratigraphy, for the same historical civilization game and exact aesthetic as the reference. Sparse matte gouache on warm ivory fibrous paper, broad unpainted space in the upper half, restricted ochre, terracotta, olive charcoal and slate palette, soft flat forms and dry irregular edges. The subject is an exposed low ravine wall with clearly legible layers: pale sandstone, a thin dark bed, muted rust-colored earth and another pale bed, interrupted by a small eroded notch. The rock bands stretch horizontally across the lower third and curve subtly with the terrain; no giant photorealistic cliff. Two very small simplified human figures stand at its left base, one pointing to a continuous band and the other recording its position on a small plain board. Their scale makes the sequence of layers the main subject, quiet observation of earth history. No large foreground portrait, no text, no dates, no arrows, no diagrams, no fossils drawn as icons, no border or watermark. Preserve the reference's handmade paper, elegant negative space and muted silhouette treatment. Finished standalone square technology card image.

## Mineral Streak Tests

Create one new square illustration for the technology Mineral Streak Tests in the same historical civilization game. Match the reference's exact aesthetic: sparse matte gouache on warm ivory fibrous paper, subdued olive charcoal, ochre, terracotta and slate, flat simplified faceless figures, irregular dry edges and expansive upper 60 percent of untouched paper. One adult woman mineral worker sits alone beside a low pale rock slab in the lower left third. She rubs a small dark ore fragment across a light unglazed ceramic tile, leaving a clear short rust-red powder streak; a separate black powder streak and two untested stone specimens are visible nearby. Her other hand steadies the tile. The material comparison and modest hands-on action are the focal point. Simple cream linen clothing with a muted terracotta wrap, a small reed sample basket, faint low distant olive scrub. No giant foreground hands, no portrait face, no companion, no large workshop, no roof. No letters, labels, arrows, numeric scales, borders, watermark, photography, glossy texture or modern laboratory tools. Quiet observation, distinctive lower-left composition, finished standalone square research card matching the series.
