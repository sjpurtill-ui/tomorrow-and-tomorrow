The previous native planet-edge capture exposed real rendering errors near x = 20,037.5 km. Interpolated absolute coordinates lost sub-metre precision: screen derivatives alternated between zero and large steps, texture UVs quantized into vertical bands, and large wave angles lost their phase. An initial analytical flat-plane comparison reproduced the filter error independently of terrain generation. Before/after images confirm that correcting derivatives alone did not remove all texture striping; the final correction also preserves texture/noise phase in a nearby coordinate frame.

Ground and water calculate pixel footprints from interpolated camera-relative coordinates. Repeating ground/forest textures use nearby 64 km coordinate frames with integer rational phase reduction, preserving the existing scales and rotations. Fine material noise retains its global cell identity while interpolating small fractions. Water uses matching local bed coordinates and double-precision phase reduction before uploading small wave angles; both global and regional water receive the same phases. These are visual coordinates only, with no changes to physical positions, elevation, climate or resources.

The real far-world capture also reproduced a camera orientation defect. At near-vertical angles, adding the tiny horizontal camera offset to a large world position rounded it away. `look_at` then encountered a collinear up vector and lost the requested yaw. The regression failed six assertions against the previous implementation. Orientation now comes directly from the requested yaw/pitch basis; camera position, view distances, field of view and near/far planes are preserved. The fix matches existing oblique orientations and remains stable in all tested distant quadrants.
