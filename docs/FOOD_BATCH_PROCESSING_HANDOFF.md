Fifteen discoveries add paid mortar/dehulling equipment, dough forming, leavening, starter culture maintenance, baking, starch washing with edible residue, and eight lot-specific observation/packing methods. Existing milled flour goes directly to dough; it does not require redundant pounding. Flatbread baking requires shaping and kiln control, not leavening. Each installed tool has one finite daily throughput shared across all lots, and all operations share at most 20% of Logistics time remaining after meal preparation and grain handling. Preservation receives the remaining actual labor. Player and rival stewards make the same paid, input-aware installations, one per day with a week's food reserve.

All food comes from the existing cereal ledger. Shaping uses water; washing retains starch and edible residue with a handling loss; baking consumes fuel. Fermentation holds food for two days, shortened to one only when a real matured starter portion is consumed. Maintaining starter uses actual food and water; starter remains unavailable as ordinary rations and spoils. Processing commitments are limited to surplus. Ready stocks enter the same food consumption, external issue, storage, spoilage, forecast and net-stock accounting; unfinished work consumes storage but is unavailable to eat. Baked food contributes to the existing small prepared-food quality share only when eaten. There is no milk or herd production in this release.

Traceability, loss measurement, humidity, acidity, water activity, process review, barrier testing and leak detection operate on bounded lots, consume workers/supplies and representative samples, and never mark unexamined portions. Packaging needs a reviewed lot, paid material and a passing leak test. Protection expires after three days, seals deteriorate, and repackaging pays again and invalidates the old leak result. The inspector shows installed capacity, available/in-process rations, waiting lots and observations, with a real paid installation button.
