# Security: years 1200–1800

**Scope.** The game's **Security** research line (`security` dynamic; `direction: "Warfare"` entries in `society_knowledge_catalog.gd`, `resource_knowledge_catalog.gd`, `armor_knowledge.gd`, `joint_force_knowledge.gd`, `military_education_knowledge.gd` and `combined_arms_doctrine.gd`) continues `SECURITY_600_1200.md` and `registry_1200.json`. Its four channels are Military readiness, Organized defense, Public safety and Crisis resilience. Each discovery changes **what generals can do**: the unit kinds, formations, siege methods, fortifications and fleets they can choose from. Generals make every battlefield decision. The player never controls units. They give objectives in conversation and see the consequences (`docs/GENERAL_CAMPAIGN_DESIGN.md`). Names are generic practices. Real history is used only to calibrate dates.

**Historical anchor.** On the `technology_eras.gd` CURVE `[[800,-500],[1500,1000],[2000,1600]]`, game 1200 is about AD 360, 1300 about AD 570, 1400 about AD 790, 1500 about AD 1000, 1600 about AD 1120, 1700 about AD 1240 and 1800 about AD 1360. In years 1200–1500 one game year is about 2.14 historical years. In 1500–1800 it is about 1.2 historical years, so discoveries crowd more closely in the second table. The plausible bands are:
- **Cavalry:** paired stirrups come at about 1300 and the couched lance at 1515. Mail from head to foot follows at 1627 and the great helm at 1686. Plate appears only at the end: the coat of plates at 1742, plate limb defences at 1778 and the visored helmet at 1784.
- **Fortification:** triple land walls come at 1220, the motte at 1478 and the stone keep at 1525. Round flanking towers follow at 1654, machicolations at 1698 and concentric castles at 1727.
- **Siege engines:** the hybrid engine comes at 1605, `counterweight_engines` at 1667 and wall crossbow engines at 1732.
- **Infantry:** crossbow spanning improves in three steps (1565, 1747, 1781). Town pike hedges come at 1752 and massed self-bow volleys at 1765. The drilled pike square (`pike_drill`) belongs later.
- **Fire and powder:** siphoned liquid fire (1345) is a petroleum incendiary. Nitre incendiaries follow at 1760. `black_powder` arrives at 1787, as chemistry only. Decisive gunpowder weapons (`powder_artillery`, hand cannon, matchlocks) belong later.

**Research time.** Time is given in game years while a staffed Security team is working on the item. Real minutes are for **1 day/s**: 1 game year is about 6 minutes, and 10 game years are about 1 hour. At 3 days/s, divide by 3.

**Id column.** A plain `id` is in the main catalog today; its year comes from `HISTORICAL_YEAR` through the CURVE. `NEW` = not authored yet. No `NEW` slug repeats an id in `docs/research/registry.json` (0–600), `docs/research/y600/registry_1200.json` (600–1200), `scripts/*.gd` or another 1200–1800 list. "(continues: id)" names an earlier registry item that the row improves. "(shared: X)" marks an item that straddles into line X. Bands are ±40 years, ±30 for key thresholds. Metal and armor production steps belong to Production.

**"Today" column.** The earliest year seen in recorded headless campaigns (`docs/technology-review/pacing/*.json`). It is `n/s` when the item is in main but was not reached in any recorded run, and `—` when it is not in main.

The total is **77** advances: 30 in years 1200–1500 and 47 in years 1500–1800. Across the four channels, one lands about every 8 years. Years 1200–1500 have fewer items, but they are longer: state-scale walls, military districts and fortified-town networks. Years 1700–1800 are densest, because castles, armor, infantry and powder chemistry all change in the historical span AD 1250–1360.

**Key thresholds (and what generals gain):**
1. **Cavalry and armor:** remount school (`mounted_remount_school` 1250) → paired stirrups (1300) → kite-shield riders (1490) → couched lance (1515) → full mail (1627) → great helm (1686) → coat of plates (1742) → visored helmet (1784). Generals gain a shock charge that breaks loose infantry, and riders who survive archery.
2. **Fortification:** hilltop citadel towns (1208) → triple land walls (1220) → burgh network (1440) → motte and bailey (1478) → stone keep (1525) → curtain walls (1575) → round towers (1654) → machicolations (1698) → concentric castles (1727). A defending general can hold out longer with fewer troops. A besieging general needs engines, blockade forts and months.
3. **Siege:** hybrid engine (1605) → blockade forts (1633) → `counterweight_engines` (1667) → captured engineer corps (1710) → wall crossbow engines (1732). Generals can breach stone walls instead of only starving the defenders out.
4. **Organized force:** military district settlement (1245) → generals' field manual (1290) → muster rolls (1370) → mounted retinues by summons (1390) → castle-guard rotations (1405) → rotating half-levy (1448) → service money for hired troops (1612) → decimal army (1673) → free companies (1793). Generals get larger, more predictable musters and a written doctrine to argue from. Fiefs and march lords are Institutions (1432, 1446).
5. **Infantry answers to cavalry:** stirrup crossbow (1565) → compulsory archery (1712) → pike hedge (1752) → polearms (1757) → massed self-bow volleys (1765).
6. **Fleets:** chain booms (1275) → liquid fire (1345) → spur galleys (1358) → river raiding ships (1380) → series-built galleys (1583) → `ocean_sailing` (1692) → ship castles (1704) → pressed merchant fleets (1722). Horse transports are Logistics (1548). The state shipyard payroll is Labor (1598).
7. **Public safety and crisis:** raid-season refuges (1322) → realm beacon chain (1420) → hearth-cover curfew (1545) → hue and cry (1598) → fireproof roofs (1660) → ward night watch (1716) → roadside clearance (1737) → plague cordons (1799). These belong to other lines:
   - **Knowledge:** the timed beacon code (1432).
   - **Institutions:** holy truce days (1510) and keepers of the peace (1776).
   - **Infrastructure:** stone party walls (1742).
   - **Health:** isolation of arriving ships and travellers (1797).
   - **Culture:** heraldic arms (1620). Tournaments are here (1647).
8. **Powder chemistry:** nitre incendiaries (1760) → `black_powder` (1787). Nitre beds are Ecology (1775).

**Government and civic life.** These rows should change offices, law or buildings at the seat of rule:
- **Military district settlement** (1245) puts district generals in charge of both civil and military rule.
- **Mounted retinues by summons** (1390) fill the court with sworn landholding riders, and the ruler moves into a hall among them.
- **Muster rolls** (1370) add muster clerks. **Castle-guard rotations** (1405) add castellans' duty lists.
- The **motte** (1478), **stone keep** (1525) and **concentric castle** (1727) turn the seat of rule itself from an open hall into a fortress.
- The **oath-sworn foreign household guard** (1425) changes who stands nearest the ruler.
- **Arms export bans** (1330), **hue and cry** (1598), **fireproof roofs** (1660) and **compulsory archery** (1712) are new laws.
- The **generals' field manual** (1290) gives generals a written doctrine they can cite in conversation.
