# Health: years 1200–1800

**Scope.** The **Health** research line (`health` dynamic; `direction: "Wellbeing"`), covering treatment, surgery, drugs and pharmacy, hospitals and nursing, the licensing of healers, sanitation and the response to pestilence. Birth, infant care and children's medicine belong to Demography and are marked shared where they touch Health. Street paving, water conduits and drains belong to Infrastructure. Continues `docs/research/y600/HEALTH_600_1200.md`; nothing below duplicates `docs/research/registry.json` or `docs/research/y600/registry_1200.json`, and "(continues: id)" names the earlier item.

**Historical anchor.** `scripts/technology_eras.gd` CURVE `[[800,-500],[1500,1000],[2000,1600]]`: game 1200 ≈ AD 360, 1300 ≈ AD 570, 1400 ≈ AD 790, 1500 ≈ AD 1000, 1600 ≈ AD 1120, 1700 ≈ AD 1240, 1800 ≈ AD 1360. Years 1200–1500 run at ≈ 2.14 historical years per game year (late antiquity through the early medieval world); years 1500–1800 run at 1.2 (the high-medieval expansion, ending just before the great mid-century pestilence runs its course and before gunpowder is decisive). Names are generic alternative-history practices; real places, people, states and religions are calibration only. (regional) items suit a monsoon-river, east-continental or hot dry-land climate or tradition and should be gated by terrain or culture.

**Research time.** Game years of staffed research on the item. Real minutes are for **1 day/s** (1 game year ≈ 6 min; 6 y ≈ 37 min; 10 y ≈ 61 min; 20 y ≈ 2 h). At 3 days/s, divide by 3.

**Id column.** A plain id is in the main catalog today. `NEW · slug` = not authored yet. No slug repeats an id in `docs/research/registry.json` (0–600), `docs/research/y600/registry_1200.json` (600–1200), `scripts/*.gd` or `technology_eras.gd` `HISTORICAL_YEAR`. "(continues: id)" names the earlier item a row improves. `(shared: X)` straddles another line; `(culture)` feeds this line from Culture.

**"Today" column.** Earliest year seen in recorded headless campaigns (`docs/technology-review/pacing/*.json`); `—` for items not in main.

## Pacing

| Years | 1200–1300 | 1300–1400 | 1400–1500 | 1500–1600 | 1600–1700 | 1700–1800 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Health advances | 7 | 10 | 16 | 11 | 9 | 19 |

The total is **72** advances: 34 in 1200–1500 and 38 in 1500–1800, one about every 8.3 years. Simple remedies take 4–6 years. Institutions and texts take 8–12 years, and the 14 key thresholds take 6–20 years (the teaching canon is 2 h). The line moves from charity hospitals and compiled texts to ward hospitals, licensing and bedside teaching (1420–1500), then to faculties, apothecary law and inquests (1645–1706). It ends with the first organised answer to a great pestilence: health boards, gate watches and cordons, paid plague physicians and a thirty-day isolation of arrivals.

**Key thresholds:**
1. **Hospitals:** charity hospitals (600–1200: 1195) → ruled hospital staff (1218) → ward hospitals with pharmacy (1420) → bedside teaching (1470) → nursing brotherhoods (1588) → admission books (1690).
2. **Licensing and law:** palace physician (1210) → court medical office (1328, regional) → licensed drug shops (1380) → physician examination (1466) → faculty licence (1645) → apothecary separation (1700) → inquest handbook (1706) → court autopsy (1750).
3. **Texts and observation:** excerpted collections (1205) → practitioner compendium (1338) → pox and measles told apart (1458) → compared treatment groups (1462) → teaching canon (1521) → contact contagion (1526) → pulmonary transit (1703).
4. **Surgery:** eye treatise (1445) → illustrated surgery (1500) → gut-string sutures (1515) → hollow-needle cataract (1532) → dry wound dressing (1722) → public dissection (1765) → school surgery manual (1782).
5. **Pestilence:** swelling plague recognised (1292) → lime graves and bedding burned (1298–1304) → street and slaughter ordinances (1745–1772) → health boards, gate watch and cordons, and paid plague physicians (1790–1794) → thirty-day isolation (1797).
6. **Drugs:** illustrated herbals (1275) → hospital formulary (1436) → sugar syrups (1440) → state-printed pharmacopoeia (1490, regional) → distilled waters (1505) → adulteration tests (1510) → town compound book (1632).

Ground lenses are **Knowledge**'s `optical_lenses` (1733); reading spectacles (1740) are placed here. Soap is **Production**'s (`salted_hard_soap` 600–1200, `soap_manufacture` 1625). Latrine towers are **Infrastructure**'s (`latrine_chute_towers`, 1606). Street filth ordinances, slaughter outside the walls, gate watches and cordons in pestilence, and the isolation of arrivals stay in this line; **Security** supplies the guards and **Ecology** the river side.

**Government and civic life.** These should visibly change government and civic life in the civic-evolution pass:
- **palace_physician_office (1210):** a chief physician joins the court composition as a standing office.
- **medical_office_specialties (1328)** and **physician_licensing_exam (1466):** a medical office with examiners appears. Healers need a licence, so the court or town gains a licensing board.
- **ward_hospitals (1420)** and **nursing_brotherhoods (1588):** the town's largest civic building after the seat of rule becomes a ward hospital with a master or warden.
- **apothecary_separation (1700)**, **drug_adulteration_tests (1510)** and **spoiled_food_condemnation (1680):** market inspectors and sworn apothecaries become town officers. Price schedules are posted.
- **inquest_handbook (1706)** and **court_autopsy (1750):** an inquest officer (coroner) is attached to the courts, and deaths become matters of law.
- **pestilence_health_boards (1790)** and **arrival_isolation_period (1797):** in pestilence years a temporary health magistracy overrides ordinary officials. A harbour isolation station appears, and armed cordons close the gates.
