# Nutrition: years 1200–1800

**Scope.** The **Nutrition** research line (`nutrition` dynamic; `direction: "Sustenance"`), channels Daily supply, Diet quality, Land productivity and Stored reserve. It covers field systems and ploughs, new crops and orchards, dairy, fish and meat preserving, brewing and distilling, bread and its regulation, estate accounting of yields, and public grain against dearth. Mill machinery (water, tide and wind), iron tools and horse harness belong to Production and Logistics, and mill and labour dues belong to Labor. Common-land and forest management belong to Ecology. Continues `docs/research/y600/NUTRITION_600_1200.md`; nothing below duplicates `docs/research/registry.json` or `docs/research/y600/registry_1200.json`, and "(continues: id)" names the earlier item.

**Historical anchor.** `scripts/technology_eras.gd` CURVE `[[800,-500],[1500,1000],[2000,1600]]`: game 1200 ≈ AD 360, 1300 ≈ AD 570, 1400 ≈ AD 790, 1500 ≈ AD 1000, 1600 ≈ AD 1120, 1700 ≈ AD 1240, 1800 ≈ AD 1360. Years 1200–1500 run at ≈ 2.14 historical years per game year (late antiquity through the early medieval world); years 1500–1800 run at 1.2 (the high-medieval expansion, ending just before the great mid-century pestilence runs its course and before gunpowder is decisive). Names are generic alternative-history practices; real places, people, states and religions are calibration only. (regional) items suit a monsoon-river, east-continental or hot dry-land climate or tradition and should be gated by terrain or culture.

**Research time.** Game years of staffed research on the item. Real minutes are for **1 day/s** (1 game year ≈ 6 min; 6 y ≈ 37 min; 10 y ≈ 61 min; 20 y ≈ 2 h). At 3 days/s, divide by 3.

**Id column.** A plain id is in the main catalog today. `NEW · slug` = not authored yet. No slug repeats an id in `docs/research/registry.json` (0–600), `docs/research/y600/registry_1200.json` (600–1200), `scripts/*.gd` or `technology_eras.gd` `HISTORICAL_YEAR`. "(continues: id)" names the earlier item a row improves. `(shared: X)` straddles another line; `(culture)` feeds this line from Culture.

**"Today" column.** Earliest year seen in recorded headless campaigns (`docs/technology-review/pacing/*.json`); `—` for items not in main.

The total is **75** advances: 35 in 1200–1500 and 40 in 1500–1800, one about every 8.0 years; with four channels, Nutrition always has an age-appropriate item open. Most steps take 4–10 years; the 8 key thresholds take 8–15 years (49–91 min). The early centuries are about late-antique estates holding on and new warm-climate crops; the middle is the heavy-plough and three-field package; the last centuries are about markets, preserved trade foods and the regulation of bread.

**Key thresholds:**
1. **Tillage and fields:** coulter plough (600–1200: 1100) → mouldboard plough (1400) → three-field rotation (1430) → ridge-and-furrow (1438) → spring-field legumes (1445) → spiked harrow (1478) → horse ploughing (1560) → marl and lime (1696). Pooled plough teams are owned by **Labor** (`shared_plough_teams`, 1430). Ridge-and-furrow drainage stays here (1438). The horse collar and horseshoes are owned by **Logistics** (`rigid_horse_collar`, 1300; `nailed_horseshoes`, 1455).
2. **Warm-climate package (regional):** irrigated cane (1345) → terraced rice (1370) → citrus and new vegetables (1450–1455) → dry-land rice basins (1465) → quick-ripening rice (1510) → refined loaf sugar (1545).
3. **Preserved and traded food:** stockfish (1470) → offshore cod (1520) → keg butter (1682) → aged cheese wheels (1700) → hopped export beer (1715) → spirits (1722) → barrelled salt meat (1742) → barrel-packed herring (1770).
4. **Bread and public food:** grain-drying kilns (1228) → dearth price ceilings (1415) → common bake-ovens (1482) → graded loaves (1600) → loaf-weight assize (1690) → licensed alehouses (1725) → town dearth granaries (1785). The price-steadying granary (600–1200) is owned by **Institutions**.
5. **Written husbandry:** monthly farm calendar (1220) → farm encyclopedia (1260) → dry-land husbandry treatise (1650) → yield accounts (1675) → illustrated farm treatise (1760). The estate steward's treatise is owned by **Ecology** (`steward_husbandry_treatises`, 1734).

Every mill is owned by **Production**: `floating_boat_mills` (1262), `horizontal_wheel_mills` (1275), `tide_mills` (1332), `vertical_axis_windmills` (1462) and `post_windmills` (1654). The duty to grind at the lord's mill is **Labor**'s `mill_suit_obligation` (1475). Coney warrens (1615) and protected drove routes (1728) stay here; **Ecology** handles their side effects (`warren_escape_liability`, 1690; upland overgrazing). Harvest by-laws belong to **Labor** (`harvest_bylaws` 1634).

**Government and civic life.** These should visibly change government and civic life in the civic-evolution pass:
- **dearth_price_ceilings (1415)** and **bread_weight_assize (1690):** a market or bread warden joins the town officials. Posted price and weight boards appear in the market square.
- **ale_taster_licensing (1725):** a sworn ale-taster serves as a petty town office, and licensed alehouses show a sign.
- **town_dearth_granaries (1785):** a grain office with its own granary building sits beside the seat of rule. The court gains a grain master.
- **estate_yield_accounts (1675)** and **great_aisled_barns (1640):** a steward or reeve keeps written accounts beside the estate barn, and the barn becomes the local seat of lordly collection.
- **village_bake_ovens (1482):** the lord's oven becomes a monopoly with an oven-keeper, alongside Labor's mill suit.
- **measured_court_recipes (1752):** a master cook and kitchen household join the court composition.
