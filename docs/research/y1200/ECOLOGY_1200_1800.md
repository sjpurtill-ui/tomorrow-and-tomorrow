# Ecology: years 1200–1800

**Scope.** The game's **Ecology** research line (`ecology` dynamic; `Nature`-direction entries and the geoscience and field-botany catalogs) continues `ECOLOGY_600_1200.md` and `registry_1200.json`. It covers woodland and fuel, land, soil and water, herds and pasture, wildlife and fisheries, pollution, and climate signals. In this window ecology becomes law: forest courts, stinted commons, dike boards, water tribunals, and river and smoke ordinances. These belong to other lines:
- **Nutrition:** crop rotation, ploughs, fish ponds and sheep folding.
- **Infrastructure:** estuary embankments, tide gates and town water supply.
- **Production:** coal-fired ironworking.
- **Health:** street filth, slaughterhouses, plague and quarantine.
- **Knowledge:** rain gauges.

Names are generic practices. Real history is used only to calibrate dates.

**Historical anchor.** On the `technology_eras.gd` CURVE `[[800,-500],[1500,1000],[2000,1600]]`, game 1200 is about AD 360, 1300 about AD 570, 1400 about AD 790, 1500 about AD 1000, 1600 about AD 1120, 1700 about AD 1240 and 1800 about AD 1360. In years 1200–1500 one game year is about 2.14 historical years. In 1500–1800 it is about 1.2 historical years, so discoveries crowd more closely in the second table. Years 1200–1500 cover late-antique retreat and early-medieval recovery: neglected terraces and drains, regrowth over abandoned fields, runoff farming in dry lands, village wood-reeves, wolf bounties, water tribunals and licensed forest clearing. Years 1500–1800 cover the high-medieval expansion and its limits: polders, peat and sea-coal, forest law, coppice with standards, warrens and deer parks, stinted commons, sheepwalks, smoke and river ordinances, and nitre beds. The first cold-period signals come at the end.

**Research time.** Time is given in game years while a staffed Ecology team is working on the item. Real minutes are for **1 day/s**: 1 game year is about 6 minutes, and 10 game years are about 1 hour. At 3 days/s, divide by 3.

**Id column.** A plain `id` is in the main catalog today; its year comes from `HISTORICAL_YEAR` through the CURVE. `NEW` = not authored yet. No `NEW` slug repeats an id in `docs/research/registry.json` (0–600), `docs/research/y600/registry_1200.json` (600–1200), `scripts/*.gd` or another 1200–1800 list. "(continues: id)" names an earlier registry item that the row improves. "(shared: X)" marks an item that straddles into line X. Bands are ±40 years, ±30 for key thresholds.

**"Today" column.** The earliest year seen in recorded headless campaigns (`docs/technology-review/pacing/*.json`). It is `n/s` when the item is in main but was not reached in any recorded run, and `—` when it is not in main.

The total is **75** advances: 31 in years 1200–1500 and 44 in years 1500–1800. Across the six channels, one lands about every 8 years. Most items are short customs and ordinances (5–8 years). Long items (10–14 years) are courts, charters and state-scale reclamation, and they are the key thresholds. The last century is densest, because fuel, grazing and town pollution all reach their limits together.

**Key thresholds:**
1. **Woodland and fuel:** village wood-reeves (1340) → fixed charcoal hearths (1360) → licensed forest clearing (1497) → peat fuel (1508) → forest perambulation (1580) → forest-law courts (1592) → coppice with standards (1652) → sea-coal (1688) → ironmaster coppices (1718) → coal-smoke limits (1755) → felling rotations (1762) → conifer sowing (1798). Coal-fired ironworking is Production (1525).
2. **Land and water:** terrace collapse (1210) → runoff fields (1222) → endowed reclamation (1325) → ridge-and-furrow drainage (1425) → water tribunals (1450) → storm-surge records (1475) → sluiced polders (1555) → dike boards (1700) → drained-land subsidence (1740) → avalanche-guard forests (1790) → drowned land given back (1792). Estuary embankments and tide gates are Infrastructure (1498, 1590). Each landholder's dike duty is Labor (1644).
3. **Herds and pasture:** summer shielings (1355) → quickset hedges (1370) → managed heathland (1385) → common fallow grazing (1488) → stinted commons (1692) → wool-flock overgrazing (1705) → chartered sheepwalks (1722).
4. **Wildlife and fisheries:** estuary weirs (1295) → reserved hunting (1395) → wolf bounties (1400) → fur beasts trapped out (1430) → hawk eyries (1518) → rabbit warrens (1612) → fishery wardens (1615) → deer parks (1622) → relict wild cattle (1662) → shellfish seasons (1715) → salmon gaps (1760).
5. **Pollution and towns:** hilltop smelting hearths (1530) → mine-water complaints (1672) → kiln fuel licences (1678) → downstream trade quarters (1682) → river offal bans (1750) → coal-smoke limits (1755). Street filth ordinances (1745) and slaughter outside the walls (1772) are Health.
6. **Climate signals:** dimmed-sun years (1280) → storm-surge records (1475) → crop-limit shifts (1695) → wet-summer retreat (1780) → glacier advance (1785) → daily weather diaries (1795). Rain gauges are Knowledge (1706).
7. **Nitre:** nitre earth leaching (1745) → `nitrate_cultivation` nitre beds (1775) → Security `black_powder` (1787, chemistry only).

**Government and civic life.** These rows should change offices, law or buildings at the seat of rule:
- **Forest-law courts** (1592) add forest justices, wardens, a separate forest law and a hunting lodge for the ruler.
- **Irrigation water tribunals** (1450) add a weekly water court held in public.
- **Dike boards** (1700) add an elected dike reeve, dike levies and a dike court.
- **Stinted commons** (1692) add written village byelaws enforced by a manor court.
- **River offal bans** (1750) and **coal-smoke limits** (1755) are the first town ordinances on river and air, with fines and inspectors.
- **Forest felling ordinances** (1762) and **fishery wardens** (1615) add officers who answer to the ruler's forest and river courts.
- **Reserved hunting rights** (1395) and **paled deer parks** (1622) set the lord's park beside the seat.
- **Chartered sheepwalks** (1722) give the flock-owners' council a charter and its own judges on the drove routes.

The great survey is Demography (`realm_holding_survey`, 1572).
