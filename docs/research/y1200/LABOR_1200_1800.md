# Labor: years 1200–1800

**Scope.** The game's **Labor** research line covers how work is organized, paid, timed, taught and bargained over: crews, wages and shares, contracts, workshops, apprenticeship, guilds, and the duties that bind workers to land, trade or state. This list continues `docs/research/y600/LABOR_600_1200.md` (which ends with land-bound tenancy, rule-bound work communities and labor dues commuted to money). Names describe generic practices. Real history is used only to calibrate timing. Offices and law that merely *use* labor (courts, charters, taxes) belong to Institutions; population movement belongs to Demography.

**Historical anchor.** `CURVE` in `scripts/technology_eras.gd` (`[[800,-500],[1500,1000],[2000,1600]]`, read from `origin/codex/research-600`) maps game 1200 ≈ AD 360, 1300 ≈ 570, 1400 ≈ 790, 1500 ≈ 1000, 1600 ≈ 1120, 1700 ≈ 1240 and 1800 ≈ 1360. Between 1200 and 1500, one game year is about 2.1 real years; between 1500 and 1800 it is 1.2, so the high-medieval centuries are twice as dense per game year. The calibration sources run in order:
- late-antique hereditary corporations, tenants under patrons, state workshops and convict gangs (1200–1300)
- early-medieval estates with week-work, boon-work and bound estate craftsmen; share contracts for herds, sheaves and catches (1300–1490)
- hired-labor levies, apprentice indentures, the cloth craft chain and state shipyards (1500–1600)
- high-medieval guild regulation, masterpieces, journeymen, putting-out and wage conflict (1600–1775)
- labor statutes after mass deaths at the end of the window (≈ 1790, AD 1350)

**Research time.** Times are game years of work by a staffed Labor team. Real minutes are for **1 day/s** (speed setting 4): 1 game year takes about 6 real minutes, and 10 game years take about 1 hour.

**Id column.** A plain id is in the main catalog today (`scripts/*.gd`); its catalog year is in `HISTORICAL_YEAR`. `NEW` = not authored yet. No `NEW` slug repeats an id in `docs/research/registry.json` (0–600), `docs/research/y600/registry_1200.json` (600–1200) or a quoted id in `scripts/*.gd`. "(continues: id)" names the earlier registry item, or an item in this list, that a row improves.

**"Today" column.** The earliest year seen in recorded headless campaigns (`docs/technology-review/pacing/*.json`). `n/r` = in the catalog but never reached in a recorded run. `—` = not in main.

The total is **86** advances: 33 in 1200–1500 and 53 in 1500–1800, with 0 catalog ids and 86 new items. 12 rows are key thresholds. The first half is thinner because each game year there covers about 2.1 real years of slower early-medieval change; after 1500 one game year is 1.2 real years and the high-medieval expansion lands one advance about every 6 years (every 9 before 1500). Short items take 4–6 years; the long thresholds take 12–12 years (73–73 real minutes).

**Key thresholds:**
1. **Binding and freedom:** patronage (1228) → runaway workers returned (1250) → **settled bond-servants (1292)** → **week-work (1318)** → manumission charters (1495) → clearance dues holiday (1528) → **a year and a day in a chartered town (1616)** → purchased freedom (1664) → money rent (1740) → **labor statutes after mass deaths (1790)** → laborer mobility (1797). Tenants bound by law are Institutions' (`bound_estate_tenants`, 1212); the unfree status of children is Demography's (`hereditary_unfree_status`, 1440).
2. **Estates and duties:** week-work (1318) and boon-work (1330) → estate craftsmen (1335) → **estate survey books (1380)** → stewards' audits (1410) → shared plough teams (1430) → mill suit (1475) → **service replaced by a hiring levy (1555)** → village reeve (1628) → harvest by-laws (1634). Estate carrying services and bridge duty are Logistics' (`estate_carrying_services` 1308, `landholder_bridge_duty` 1420).
3. **Guilds as work bodies:** craft streets (1392) → **apprentice indentures (1535)** → craft guilds (`craft_guilds`, owned by Institutions at 1583) → trade monopoly (1600) → single-craft rule (1605) → searchers (1622) → journeyman passes (1640) → **masterpiece (1692)** → **town book of trades (1712)** → women's guilds (1728) → closed mastership (1772) → **guild seats on the town council (1775)**. The governor's trade rulebook (`prefect_guild_rulebook`, 1459) and town statute books (`revised_town_statute_books`, 1702) are Institutions'.
4. **Pay and bargaining:** grain shares (1368) → herd and sheaf shares (1395–1442) → catch and voyage shares (1482–1512) → shearing by the fleece (1615) → seasonal day rates (1658) → carvers' piece rates (1682) → guild-fixed journeyman rates (1698) → walkouts (1746) → joint rate boards (1760) → truck ban (1780).
5. **Scale of work:** city-supply corporations (1215) → state artisans (1240) → cloth craft chain (1520) → **state shipyard payroll (1598)** → debt-bound putting-out (1705) → rented looms (1735) → rural putting-out beyond guild reach (1762).
6. **Time:** communal bell hours (1425) → night-work bans (1718) → town work bell (1755). Clocks are Knowledge's (`striking_equal_hour_clock`, 1775).

**Government and civic life.** These discoveries should visibly change the court and the seat of rule:
- `week_work_service` (1318), `estate_survey_books` (1380) and `estate_steward_audits` (1410): the lord's household gains a steward, bailiffs and a clerk of the rolls. Audits of the estates are held at court.
- `village_reeve_office` (1628): each village has a reeve who answers to the lord's steward.
- `hired_labor_levy` (1555): the treasury collects a levy and hires workers for walls, roads and palaces. The court no longer summons villagers for works.
- `state_shipyard_workforce` (1598) and `weekly_works_payrolls` (1686): a clerk of the works and a shipyard master join the officials who report to the ruler.
- `town_residence_freedom` (1616): chartered towns become a separate estate with their own law, and bound tenants can escape to them.
- `town_trade_statute_book` (1712) and `guild_council_seats` (1775): guild masters sit on the town council. The council chamber becomes a second seat of civic power beside the ruler's hall.
- `post_plague_labor_statutes` (1790): justices of labor enforce wage law across the realm. Wages become a matter the ruler legislates on.
