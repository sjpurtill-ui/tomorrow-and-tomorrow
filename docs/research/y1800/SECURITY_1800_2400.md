# Security: years 1800–2400

**Scope.** The game's **Security** research line (`security` dynamic; `direction: "Warfare"` entries in `society_knowledge_catalog.gd`, `resource_knowledge_catalog.gd`, `armor_knowledge.gd`, `joint_force_knowledge.gd`, `military_education_knowledge.gd` and `combined_arms_doctrine.gd`) continues `y1200/SECURITY_1200_1800.md` and `registry_1800.json`. Its four channels are Military readiness, Organized defense, Public safety and Crisis resilience.

Each discovery changes **what generals can do**: which unit kinds, weapons, formations, siege methods, fortifications and fleets they can choose from. Generals make every battlefield decision. The player never controls units. The player gives objectives in conversation and sees the consequences (`docs/GENERAL_CAMPAIGN_DESIGN.md`). Metal, powder-mill and gun-founding production steps belong to Production and are marked shared. Names are generic practices. Real history is used only to calibrate dates. Rows marked `[gov: …]` change offices, law, towns, the seat or the court.

**Historical anchor.** The `technology_eras.gd` CURVE on `origin/codex/research-600` runs `[[1500,1000],[2000,1600],[2400,1800]]` through this window. Game 1800 is about AD 1360, 1900 about AD 1480, 2000 about AD 1600, 2100 about AD 1650, 2200 about AD 1700, 2300 about AD 1750 and 2400 about AD 1800. In years 1800–2000, one game year is about 1.2 historical years. In years 2000–2400 it is half a historical year.

The gunpowder transition in this window runs in this order:
- **Hand guns:** fire lances (1800) → pot guns (1806) → **hand-gun tubes** (1822) → matchlock arquebus drill (1920) → wheel-lock (1932) → snaphance (1979) → **flintlock** (2026) → plug bayonet (2076) → socket bayonet (2182) → rifles for picked marksmen (2290).
- **Artillery:** great bombards (`powder_artillery` 1848) → wheeled siege train (1902) → regimental guns (2062) → solid-bored cannon (2224) → a standard field-artillery system (2332).
- **Fortification:** keyhole gunports (1817) → gun towers (1878) → earth bulwarks (1896) → **angled bastion trace** (1942) → ravelins and outworks (2054) → fortress frontier belt (2160). Siege answers keep pace: zig-zag approaches (1994), parallels (2148) and ricochet fire (2176).
- **Organized force:** standing horse companies (1872) → pike squares (1884) → pike-and-shot regiments (1946) → countermarch volley and printed drill (2002, 2014) → **standing crown army** (2100) → staff planning (2320) → all-arms divisions (2360) → the whole nation in arms (2386).

**Research time.** Time is given in game years while a staffed Security team is working on the item. Real time is for **1 day/s**: 1 game year is about 6 minutes, and 10 game years are about 1 hour. At 3 days/s, divide by 3.

**Id column.** A plain `id` is in the main catalog today. Its year comes from `HISTORICAL_YEAR` through the CURVE, unless the last table gives a correction. `NEW` = not authored yet. No `NEW` slug repeats an id in `docs/research/registry.json` (0–600), `y600/registry_1200.json` (600–1200), `y1200/registry_1800.json` (1200–1800), the game's baked blocks, `scripts/*.gd`, or another 1800–2400 list. "(continues: id)" names an earlier item that the row improves. "(shared: X)" marks an item that straddles into line X. Bands are ±40 years, and ±30 for key thresholds (bold).

**"Today" column.** The earliest year seen in recorded headless campaigns (`docs/technology-review/pacing/*.json`). It is `n/s` when the item is in main but was not reached in any recorded run, and `—` when it is not in main.

The total is **104** advances: 56 in years 1800–2100 and 48 in years 2100–2400. 25 of them are key thresholds. Across the four channels, one lands about every 6 years. Most items take 5–10 years. Weapon, fortress and army-system thresholds take 12–24 years. Years 1800–2000 are the densest, because hand guns, bombards, pike squares and the bastion all arrive in the historical span AD 1360–1600, when each game year still covers 1.2 historical years.

**Key thresholds (and what generals gain):**
1. **Hand guns:** `fire_lance_tubes` (1800) → **`hand_gun_tubes` (1822)** → **`matchlock_drill` (1920)** → `wheel_lock_firearms` (1932) → `rest_musket` (1968) → **`countermarch_volley_fire` (2002)** → **`flintlock_ignition` (2026)** → `paper_cartridges` (2046) → **`socket_bayonet` (2182)** → **`platoon_fire_battalions` (2204)** → `iron_ramrods` (2212) → `rifled_barrels` (2290). Generals first gain a frightening but inaccurate hand gun. Then comes a trained shot that can hold a line, and finally an all-musket battalion that needs no pikes.
2. **Artillery and siege:** pot guns (1806) → **`powder_artillery` (1848)** → **wheeled siege train (1902)** → fixed calibres (1962) → **regimental guns (2062)** → **parallels (2148)** → ricochet fire (2176) → solid-bored cannon (2224) → horse artillery (2326) → **artillery system (2332)** → war rockets (2378). Corned powder (`corned_powder_mills` 1851) and cast-iron shot (`cast_iron_shot_firebacks` 1830) are Production's. A besieging general can breach old walls in days. Against the bastion (1942), the besieger needs trenches and weeks.
3. **Fortification:** keyhole gunports (1817) → gun towers (1878) → earth bulwarks (1896) → **angled bastion trace (1942)** → coastal gun forts (1954) → ravelin outworks (2054) → **fortress frontier belt (2160)**. A defending general can hold a bastioned town against many times his numbers until a siege train and parallels arrive.
4. **Infantry and cavalry organization:** stake line (1835) → **standing horse companies (1872)** → **`pike_drill` (1884)** → **pike-and-shot regiments (1946)** → `mounted_firearms` pistol riders (1976) → **printed drill manual (2014)** → shallow line brigades (2068) → **crown standing army (2100)** → grenadiers (2138) → oblique order (2314) → **`military_staffs` (2320)** → **all-arms divisions (2360)** → columns behind skirmishers (2392). Generals get paid, drilled, permanent regiments. Then they get a staff that plans marches in advance, and finally divisions that march apart and fight together.
5. **Recruitment and the state:** captain contracts (1812) → parish free archers (1866) → patented mercenary regiments (1906) → trained bands (1982) → district muster lieutenants (1998) → muster commissaries (2008) → allotted soldier crofts (2166) → press gangs (2170) → barracks (2172) → cadet schools (2234) → canton rolls (2266) → **nation in arms (2386)**. Recruitment may draw on any available adults at any time. These rows change cost, speed and consent, not a ceiling (`GENERAL_CAMPAIGN_DESIGN.md`, recruitment rule).
6. **Fleets:** bow-gun galleys (1912) → `naval_gunnery` (1936) → race-built warship (1985) → fireships (1991) → sailing battle fleet (2092) → **line-ahead tactics (2116)** → rated warships (2124) → sea regiments (2128) → carronades (2350) → numbered signal flags (2364) → telegraph lines for army and fleet orders (2394). The galleon hull, copper sheathing and navigation are Logistics.
7. **Public safety and crisis:** parish constables (1826) → ward fire companies (1840) → travel passports (1950) → fire engine with air vessel (2084) → **capital police lieutenant (2134)** → fire hose (2154) → insured fire brigades (2196) → highway constabulary (2240) → customs riders (2258) → magistrate's runners (2298) → isolated powder magazines (2304) → disaster relief by troops (2310) → wanted notices (2344) → river police (2396). These belong to other lines:
   - **Health:** quarantine stations and plague lazarettos.
   - **Institutions:** courts of law, the justices' bench (`quarter_session_justices` 1801), police science (2260), the permanent army tax (1866) and the war ministry (2140).
   - **Infrastructure:** street lighting, fire-proof rebuilding after great fires and wide streets.
