# Labor: years 1800–2400

**Scope.** The game's **Labor** research line covers how work is organized, paid, timed, taught and bargained over: crews, wages and shares, contracts, workshops, apprenticeship, guilds, poor relief that sets people to work, and the duties that bind workers to land, trade or state. This list continues `docs/research/y1200/LABOR_1200_1800.md`, which ends with the labor statutes after mass deaths (1790) and laborers leaving their manors for higher wages (1797). Names describe generic practices. Real history is used only to calibrate timing. Offices and law that merely *use* labor (courts, parliaments, taxes, charters) belong to Institutions; population movement belongs to Demography; the machines themselves belong to Production.

**Historical anchor.** `CURVE` in `scripts/technology_eras.gd` (`[[1500,1000],[2000,1600],[2400,1800]]`, read from `origin/codex/research-600`) maps game 1800 ≈ AD 1360, 1900 ≈ 1480, 2000 = 1600, 2100 = 1650, 2200 = 1700, 2300 = 1750 and 2400 = 1800. Between 1800 and 2000, one game year is 1.2 real years; between 2000 and 2400 it is only 0.5, so the seventeenth and eighteenth centuries get twice as many game years as historical years and are the densest part of the list. The calibration sources run in order:
- wage enforcement, tenant leagues and journeymen's brotherhoods after the plague years (1800–1830)
- mining ordinances, arsenal sequence work, printing-house crews and the permanent wage journeyman (1840–1890)
- the second bondage on grain-export estates, vagrancy law and houses of correction (1896–1961)
- contact-gated bound labor overseas, then a realm-wide artisans' statute, manufactories and the parish poor law (1937–2036)
- crown manufactories, settlement laws, friendly societies and the workhouse test (2120–2250)
- mill hands, factory shifts, enclosure's landless laborers, guild abolition and the first laws against combinations (2238–2398)

**Research time.** Times are game years of work by a staffed Labor team. Real minutes are for **1 day/s** (speed setting 4): 1 game year takes about 6 real minutes, and 10 game years take about 1 hour. At 3 days/s, divide by 3.

**Id column.** A plain id is in the main catalog today (`scripts/*.gd`); its catalog year is in `HISTORICAL_YEAR` unless the last table gives a correction. `NEW` = not authored yet. No `NEW` slug repeats an id in `docs/research/registry.json` (0–600), `docs/research/y600/registry_1200.json` (600–1200), `docs/research/y1200/registry_1800.json` (1200–1800, merge aliases and excluded rows included), the game's baked research blocks, or a quoted id in `scripts/*.gd`. "(continues: id)" names the earlier registry item, or an item in this list, that a row improves.

**"Today" column.** The earliest year seen in recorded headless campaigns (`docs/technology-review/pacing/*.json`). `n/r` = in the catalog but never reached in a recorded run. `—` = not in main. Rows marked `[gov: …]` change government, civic life or the court; the civic-evolution pass should read them together with the Institutions list.

The total is **99** advances: 53 in 1800–2100 and 46 in 2100–2400, with 0 catalog ids and 99 new items. 14 rows are key thresholds. The late-medieval century (1800–1900) is dense, one advance about every 4–5 years, because it resolves the plague-era wage conflicts; 1900–2100 runs at about one every 6–7 years, and the last fifty years are crowded again as the mill, the bread-scale wage and the combination laws arrive together. Short items take 4–6 years; the long thresholds take 10–14 years (61–85 real minutes).

**Key thresholds:**
1. **Bondage and its end:** tenant leagues (1818) → **labor dues reimposed on export estates (1896)** → bound laborers overseas (1937, contact-gated) → plantation gangs (1950; the cane fields are Nutrition's `island_cane_plantations`) → rotating mine drafts (1966) → bound colliers (1990) → indentured passage (2036) → **campaign against the overseas trade in bound laborers (2374)** → **estate bondage abolished (2378)** → collier bonds released (2398). Estate tenancy law is Institutions'; the unfree status of children is Demography's.
2. **Poor and work:** begging badges (1830) → vagrancy returns (1908) → public work relief (1928) → houses of correction (1961) → compulsory poor rate (1977) → **parish poor law (2002)** → poor work stock (2010) → settlement removal (2126) → workhouse test (2246) → pauper mill apprentices (2360) → bread-scale wage supplements (2388). Hospitals and the general hospital for the idle poor are Health's (`general_confinement_hospital`, 2112); the town vagrant expulsions before this window are Demography's (`vagrant_expulsion`, 1765), and so are orphanages that apprentice fatherless children (`town_orphanage`, 1886).
3. **Guild to free trade:** unguilded cloth workers (1815) → wander years (1850) → permanent journeymen (1880) → women barred from the loom (1924) → **realm-wide artisans' statute (1969)** → printed wage assessments (2026) → rebuilding opened to all hands (2134) → wage assessment lapses (2332) → **edict abolishing guild monopolies (2353)**.
4. **Scale of work:** **arsenal sequence fitting (1860)** → clothiers' household workshops (1933) → **manufactory (1995)** → **crown manufactories (2132)** → crown works settlements (2170) → **mill hands under one bell (2238)** → pin-shop division (2294) → **factory shift system (2340)** → factory villages (2350) → factory fine rules (2370).
5. **Pay and bargaining:** wage justices (1804) → board and dry rates (1822) → boycotts (1826) → wage books (1893) → compositors' strike (1946) → corf piece rates (2078) → town-wide combinations (2250) → strike funds (2326) → statutory price lists (2346) → machine breaking (2364) → **general combination ban (2393)**.
6. **Land and wages:** demesne leasing (1840) → acre-rate harvest gangs (1983) → gang-master harvest crews (2014) → hedge riots (2066) → **landless wage laborers after enclosure (2344)**. Enclosure itself is Ecology's; the seed drill and the four-course rotation are Nutrition's.
7. **Ownership of overlaps:** the machines of the mill (throwing mills, water frame, jenny, mule) are Production's; this list places only the people who tend them. Canal and road building is Infrastructure's; `navvy_gangs` places the digging crews. Friendly-society law is shared with Institutions; poor rates and parish administration are shared with Institutions and Demography. Printing presses and type are Knowledge's; `printing_house_crews` (1894) follows Knowledge's `town_printing_houses` (1892) and places the shop crews. Galleys, press gangs and standing armies are Security's; `convict_galley_oarsmen` and `arsenal_sequence_fitting` are shared with it. Crown works are founded by Institutions' `mercantile_trade_council` (2128); `privileged_crown_manufactories` (2132) places their workers. Migration counts and passenger lists are Demography's; indentured passage, the settlement law, the emigration ban, servants' register offices and badged beggars are placed here, and Demography removed its matching rows. The abolition campaign is placed here; Culture removed its row.

**Removed as duplicates.** `half_share_tenancy` repeated 600–1200 `sharecropping_tenancy` (975). `bound_parish_apprentices` repeated Demography's `town_orphanage` (1886). This list was checked against all eleven other 1800–2400 lists.

**Government and civic life.** These discoveries should visibly change the court and the seat of rule:
- `quarter_session_wage_hearings` (1804): justices of the peace sit four times a year to hear wage cases. Labor disputes come to a local bench, not the ruler's hall.
- `tenant_leagues_against_bondage` (1818): the court faces organized tenant demands for the first time. The god can hear them, crush them or grant charters.
- `export_estate_labor_dues` (1896) and `estate_bondage_abolition` (2378): the great landholders' power over their tenants is first extended by law, then ended by decree. The decree adds commissioners who value and commute the dues.
- `realm_artisan_statute` (1969) and `printed_wage_assessments` (2026): wage-setting becomes a realm-wide office of the justices, with posted wage tables in every market town.
- `compulsory_poor_rate` (1977), `parish_poor_law` (2002), `settlement_removal_laws` (2126) and `workhouse_test` (2246): each parish gains overseers of the poor, and a workhouse becomes part of the town's fabric.
- `privileged_crown_manufactories` (2132): an intendant of manufactures joins the ministers, and crown works stand outside guild law.
- `trade_freedom_edict` (2353): the guild courts close; trades are licensed by the state instead.
