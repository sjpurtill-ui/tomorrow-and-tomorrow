# Labor: years 600–1200

**Scope.** The game's **Labor** research line covers how work is organized, paid, timed, taught and bargained over. That includes crews, rations and wages, contracts, workshops, rotations, work norms, and the obligations that bind workers to land or trade. This list continues the 0–600 list in `docs/research/registry.json` (line `labor`, 72 items ending at `crew_loans_between_cities`). No registry item is repeated here. An improvement of an earlier practice appears as a new item with its own name and a trailing "(continues: id)" note. Names describe generic practices. Real history is used only to calibrate timing.

**Historical anchor.** Game year 600 ≈ 1500 BC, 800 ≈ 500 BC and 1200 ≈ AD 360 (`scripts/technology_eras.gd` CURVE on `origin/codex/research-600`). Between 600 and 800, one game year is about 5 real years. Between 800 and 1200, it is about 2.1. The calibration sources run in order:
- Late Bronze Age palace ration economies and tomb-builder crews
- early Iron Age wage and contract labor
- classical-style public tenders
- late-antique hereditary trades and tenants bound to the land

**Research time.** Times are game years of work by a staffed Labor team. Real minutes are for **1 day/s** (speed setting 4): 1 game year takes about 6 real minutes, and 10 game years take about 1 hour.

**Id column.** `id` = in the main catalog today. `NEW` = not authored yet.

**"Today" column.** The earliest year seen in recorded headless campaigns (`docs/technology-review/pacing/*.json`). `n/r` = in the catalog but never reached in a recorded run. `—` = not in main.

## Pacing

| Years | 600–650 | 650–700 | 700–750 | 750–800 | 800–850 | 850–900 | 900–950 | 950–1000 | 1000–1050 | 1050–1100 | 1100–1150 | 1150–1200 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Labor advances | 7 | 8 | 8 | 8 | 7 | 6 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 5 | 3 | 7 |

The total is **74** advances: 44 in 600–900 and 30 in 900–1200. Early items are short (4–8 years). They cover palace rations, rotations and the first wages. Later items take longer (10–14 years) and change how whole groups of workers are bound, through public tenders, division of labor in the workshop, putting-out, hereditary trades and land-bound tenancy. One Labor advance lands about every 7–10 years, so a staffed Labor team almost always has work.

**Key thresholds:**
1. **Payment:** ration rolls (605) → graded rations (615) → silver wages (688) → piece rates (718) → uniform day rate (784) → decreed wages (945) → coin plus rations (1095) → wage edicts (1160) → dues commuted to money (1198).
2. **Contracting:** measured-length contracts (736) → surety-backed contracts (765) → public tenders (772) → staged acceptance (778) → contractor companies (905) → notarized contracts (1120).
3. **Workshop scale:** palace workshops (630) → household workshops (712) → owner workshops (830) → task division (838) → state manufactories (1045).
4. **Binding and freedom:** land for service (640) → term-limited debt labor (705) → sharecropping (975) → hereditary trades (1168) → land-bound tenancy (1182).
5. **Worker associations:** grievance hearings (655) → mutual-aid clubs (868) → craft associations (935) → licensed associations (1175).
