# Ecology: years 2400–3000

**Scope.** The game's **Ecology** research line (`ecology` dynamic; `Nature`-direction entries and the geoscience catalog) continues `y1200/ECOLOGY_1200_1800.md` and `y1200/registry_1800.json`; the 1800–2400 list is written in parallel in `y1800/`. It has seven channels: forests; land, soil and range; wildlife, parks and fisheries; **industrial pollution**; **climate and weather science**; the science of ecology and **conservation**; and earth science (the geoscience catalog). In this window ecology becomes a state concern: survey offices, weather services, fume inspectors, parks, environment agencies, and finally treaties between realms on ozone, climate and species. Catalog ids placed here: `mineral_streak_tests`, `sediment_provenance`, `comparative_mineral_hardness`, `lithologic_correlation`, `geologic_cross_sections`, `structural_geologic_mapping`, `soil_assays`, `systematic_channel_sampling`, `grade_tonnage_models`, `spatial_variograms`, `resource_kriging`, `orebody_block_models`, `geochemical_baselines` and `geochemical_anomaly_mapping`. The first seven were marked `belongs_later` in `registry_1800.json`. The 1800–2400 list defers all seven to this list. These belong to other lines:
- **Nutrition:** fertilizers, insecticides on crops, the agronomy catalog (`contour_cultivation`, `strip_cropping`, `cover_crop_mixtures` and `reduced_tillage` are `Sustenance`) and the soil conservation service (`soil_conservation_service`, 2760, shared with this line).
- **Infrastructure and Health:** sewers, water treatment, and the health effects of smog and fallout.
- **Production:** emission-control hardware, renewable power and fuels.
- **Culture:** the environmental movement as a popular cause.
- **Knowledge:** general physics and computing, and descent by selection (`descent_by_selection`, 2557).

Names are generic practices. Real history is used only to calibrate dates.

**Historical anchor.** On the `technology_eras.gd` CURVE `[[2400,1800],[2800,1950],[3000,2030]]` (on `origin/codex/research-600`), game 2400 is about AD 1800, 2500 about 1838, 2600 about 1875, 2700 about 1912, 2800 about 1950, 2900 about 1990 and 3000 is AD 2030, the end of the game. In years 2400–2800 one historical year spans about 2.7 game years; in 2800–3000 it spans 2.5. About 230 historical years of fast change must fit into 600 game years, so the list is dense: several advances land in most decades, and one historical year often carries two or three rows. Years 2400–2700 carry the first industrial age: geological maps, ice ages, acid fumes and dead rivers, the first fume inspectors, telegraphed weather maps, forest services, nature parks and the first estimate that burning coal warms the world. Years 2700–3000 carry dust storms and soil conservation, the ecosystem idea, killing smog and clean-air law, the carbon dioxide record, pesticide warnings, environment agencies, ozone, acid rain, climate panels and treaties, microplastics and carbon markets. The last 30 game years are near-future items: marked speculative-plausible, never science fiction.

**Research time.** Time is given in game years while a staffed Ecology team is working on the item. Real minutes are for **1 day/s**: 1 game year is about 6 minutes, and 10 game years are about 1 hour. At 3 days/s, divide by 3.

**Id column.** A plain `id` is in the main catalog today; its year comes from `HISTORICAL_YEAR` through the CURVE unless the last table gives a correction. `NEW` = not authored yet. No `NEW` slug repeats an id in `docs/research/registry.json` (0–600), `docs/research/y600/registry_1200.json` (600–1200), `docs/research/y1200/registry_1800.json` (1200–1800), `scripts/*.gd` or another 2400–3000 list. "(continues: id)" names an earlier item that the row improves. "(shared: X)" marks an item that straddles into line X. `[gov: …]` marks a row that changes government, civic life or the court. Bands are ±40 years, ±30 for key thresholds (bold), capped at 3000.

**"Today" column.** The earliest year seen in recorded headless campaigns (`docs/technology-review/pacing/*.json`). It is `n/s` when the item is in main but was not reached in any recorded run, and `—` when it is not in main.

## Government and civic life

These rows should change offices, law or buildings at the seat of rule:
