# Labor: years 2400–3000

**Scope.** The game's **Labor** research line covers how work is organized, paid, timed, taught and bargained over: the factory regime, bound and free labor, unions and employers' federations, strikes and arbitration, hours and holidays, wages and their floors, workplace injury, the organization of the shop floor, and at the end the office, the platform and the machine assistant. This list continues `docs/research/y1800/LABOR_1800_2400.md`, which ends with the two-shift spinning mill, mill fines, factory villages, the trade-freedom edict, the ban on combinations and the release of bound colliers. Names describe generic practices. Real history is used only to calibrate timing. Offices that administer the whole state's social insurance belong to Institutions (`old_age_pensions`, `unemployment_insurance`, `welfare_state`, `war_economy_boards`, `central_five_year_plan`, `civil_rights_law`), sickness insurance to Health (`worker_sickness_insurance`), and parental leave and guest workers to Demography. This line keeps the law of the workplace, trade bargaining, trade-specific schemes and work itself.

**Historical anchor.** `CURVE` in `scripts/technology_eras.gd` (`[[2400,1800],[2800,1950],[3000,2030]]`, read from `origin/codex/research-600`) maps game 2400 ≈ AD 1800, 2500 ≈ 1838, 2600 ≈ 1875, 2700 ≈ 1912, 2800 ≈ 1950, 2900 ≈ 1990 and 3000 ≈ 2030. One game year is about 0.375 historical years before 2800 and 0.4 after. The calibration sources run in order:
- factory acts for bound children, secret trade societies, machine-breaking's aftermath and the repeal of the combination ban (2408–2464)
- inspectors, the end of chattel bondage, the ten-hour day and amalgamated craft unions (2488–2536)
- legal unions, conciliation boards, liability and accident insurance, the eight-hour campaign and the end of child labor (2587–2644)
- wage boards, industrial courts, labor exchanges, time-and-motion study, the labor ministry and the assembly line (2660–2701)
- the eight-hour law, works councils, collective bargaining law, paid holidays and the national minimum wage (2717–2768)
- codetermination, equal pay, lean production and safety agencies (2803–2850)
- deregulation, offshoring, platform work, the right to disconnect and machine assistants at the 2030 frontier (2880–2996)

**Research time.** Times are game years of work by a staffed Labor team. Real minutes are for **1 day/s** (speed setting 4): 1 game year takes about 6 real minutes, and 10 game years take about 1 hour.

**Id column.** A plain id is in the main catalog today (`scripts/*.gd`); its catalog year is in `HISTORICAL_YEAR`. `NEW` = not authored yet. No `NEW` slug repeats an id in `docs/research/registry.json` (0–600), `y600/registry_1200.json` (600–1200), `y1200/registry_1800.json` (1200–1800), a quoted id in `scripts/*.gd`, or any id in the 1800–2400 lists and the other 2400–3000 lists. "(continues: id)" names the earlier registry item, a row in a 1800–2400 list, a row in another 2400–3000 line, or a row in this list that the row improves.

**"Today" column.** The earliest year seen in recorded headless campaigns (`docs/technology-review/pacing/*.json`). "not seen" means the catalog item never completed in a recorded run. It is `—` when the item is not in main.

The total is **110** advances: 54 in 2400–2700 and 56 in 2700–3000, with 2 catalog ids and 108 new items. 21 rows are key thresholds. One advance lands about every 5–6 years. The 2600–2650 band is the densest, because unions, liability law, insurance and the eight-hour campaign all arrive within one historical generation (AD 1870–1895). The 2400–2450 band is thin because the 1800–2400 list already holds the first mills, fines and the combination ban. Short items take 2–4 years; the long thresholds take 8–12 years (49–73 real minutes).

**Key thresholds:**
1. **Factory law:** **first factory act (2408)** → **factory inspectorate (2488)** → underground work limits (2507) → **ten-hour day (2525)** → machine guarding (2590) → shop hours (2617) → **child labor prohibition (2644)** → **eight-hour day law (2717)** → **occupational safety agency (2850)** → working-time rules (2908) → right to disconnect (2968) → heat-stress limits (2988). Child labor as a family and schooling matter is shared with Demography and Knowledge (`compulsory_elementary_schooling`, 2587).
2. **Binding and freedom:** master-and-servant penalties (2470) → coin wages (2483) → **chattel bondage abolished (2490)** → indentured contract migration (2500) → company towns (2560) → blacklists (2620) → penal labor camps (2765). Estate bondage ended in the 1800–2400 list (`estate_bondage_abolition`, 2378).
3. **Unions and bargaining:** secret trade societies (2430) → spinners' federation (2448) → **combination ban repealed (2464)** → **amalgamated craft unions (2536)** → district rate lists (2545) → lockouts (2585) → **legal standing for unions (2587)** → conciliation boards (2595) → picketing (2597) → general unions (2636) → industrial courts (2665) → general strike (2688) → works councils (2721) → industrial unions (2758) → **collective bargaining law (2760)** → tripartite bargaining (2795) → codetermination (2803) → public-sector unions (2828) → deregulation (2880) → platform worker status (2978).
4. **Pay and security:** coin wages (2483) → cooperatives (2512, 2580) → liability law (2613) → **accident insurance (2630)** → premium bonus (2650) → **minimum wage boards (2660)** → occupational diseases (2680) → labor exchanges (2690) → high day wage (2704) → **national minimum wage (2768)** → wartime wage controls (2782) → negotiated pensions (2810) → **equal pay (2835)** → redundancy pay (2862) → living wage (2940) → basic income pilots (2996). The state pension, unemployment insurance and welfare state are Institutions'; sickness insurance is Health's.
5. **Organization of work:** overlookers (2412) → time clocks (2632) → job costing (2641) → fatigue limits (`work_rest_limits`, 2667) → **time and motion study (`work_motion_studies`, 2696)** → **moving assembly line (2701)** → employment departments (2715) → morale studies (2740) → quality circles (2832) → **lean production (2845)** → flexitime (2843) → desk computers in offices (2885) → call centres (2905) → **platform gig work (2948)** → algorithmic management (2955) → **machine assistants take routine desk work (2990)**.
6. **Hours and leisure:** Saturday half-holiday (2540) → eight-hour campaign (2634) → eight-hour law (2717) → five-day week (2735) → paid holidays (2763) → forty-hour week (2768) → four-day week trials (2985). Culture owns the workers' festival day (`workers_festival_day`, 2637) and remote gatherings by video (`remote_video_gatherings`, 2975).
7. **Ownership of overlaps:** Institutions owns `poor_law_boards` (2491), `old_age_pensions` (2637), `unemployment_insurance` (2696), `war_economy_boards` (2704), `central_five_year_plan` (2741), `welfare_state` (2789) and `civil_rights_law` (2835). Health owns `worker_sickness_insurance` (2621). Demography owns `guest_worker_programmes` (2812) and `paid_parental_leave` (2860). Logistics owns `just_in_time_supply` (2868), which lean production uses. Production owns robots (`industrial_robots`, 2834), which `automation_retraining` answers.

**Government and civic life.** These discoveries should visibly change the court and the seat of rule:
- `first_factory_act` (2408) and `factory_inspectorate` (2488): the ruler sends inspectors into private works, and the council hears their reports.
- `chattel_bondage_abolition` (2490): compensation to owners is voted from the treasury; no one may be owned in the realm.
- `combination_ban_repeal` (2464), `union_legal_standing` (2587) and `collective_bargaining_law` (2760): union delegations come to court as recognised bodies, and a labor relations board sits beside the courts.
- `labor_statistics_bureau` (2605) and `labor_ministry` (2700): a minister of labor sits at the council table.
- `accident_insurance` (2630) and `minimum_wage_boards` (2660): wages and injuries become matters of law, with boards that sit under the ruler's authority.
- `standing_labor_conference` (2719): the realm sends delegates to a standing conference of realms on labor standards.
- `works_councils` (2721) and `board_level_codetermination` (2803): workers sit inside the governing bodies of the great works.
- `penal_labor_camps` (2765): a sober option for a harsh ruler, whose people come to dread the camps.
- `basic_income_pilots` (2996): the council tests paying every adult without condition at the end of the game.
