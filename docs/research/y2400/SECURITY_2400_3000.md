# Security: years 2400–3000

**Scope.** The game's **Security** research line (`security` dynamic; `direction: "Warfare"` entries in `joint_force_knowledge.gd`, `combined_arms_doctrine.gd`, `military_education_knowledge.gd` and `field_medicine.gd`) continues `y1200/SECURITY_1200_1800.md` and `y1200/registry_1800.json`; the 1800–2400 list is written in parallel in `y1800/`. Its four channels are Military readiness, Organized defense, Public safety and Crisis resilience. Each discovery changes **what generals can do**: the weapons, formations, fleets, air arms, staff methods and intelligence they can draw on. Generals make every battlefield decision. The player never controls units. They give objectives in conversation and see the consequences (`docs/GENERAL_CAMPAIGN_DESIGN.md`). Weapons of mass destruction appear as sober research. They do not end the game or grant victory. A general cannot use them on their own authority: their use is a decision the ruler must speak aloud, and the people and other realms remember it. Catalog ids placed here: 27. That is every `Warfare` id whose catalog year is after AD 1800, plus `range_estimation_drill` (AD 1800), which the 1800–2400 list defers to this list. The seven `field_medicine.gd` ids are also `Warfare`, but Health places them (2560–2712, shared with this line). These belong to other lines:
- **Production:** engines, airframes, `jet_propulsion`, `reactor_engineering` and steel.
- **Knowledge:** the telegraph, radio, `nuclear_fission` as physics, and computing.
- **Health:** hospitals, civilian medicine, the field-medicine catalog (2560–2712) and the treaty that protects the wounded (`neutral_wounded_convention`, 2571).
- **Institutions:** criminal courts and codes, passports (`frontier_passport_control`, 2707), the world assembly and its security council (`world_assembly_of_realms`, 2787), and the courts, data laws and audits that check the police. Town police, political police and mass interception are kept here.
- **Logistics:** the civil transport that railway mobilization and convoys use.

Names are generic practices. Real history is used only to calibrate dates.

**Historical anchor.** On the `technology_eras.gd` CURVE `[[2400,1800],[2800,1950],[3000,2030]]` (on `origin/codex/research-600`), game 2400 is about AD 1800, 2500 about 1838, 2600 about 1875, 2700 about 1912, 2800 about 1950, 2900 about 1990 and 3000 is AD 2030, the end of the game. In years 2400–2800 one historical year spans about 2.7 game years; in 2800–3000 it spans 2.5. About 230 historical years of fast change must fit into 600 game years, so the list is dense: several advances land in most decades, and one historical year often carries two or three rows. The plausible bands are:
- **Infantry fire:** percussion caps come at 2453, the expanding-base rifle bullet at 2531 and `metallic_cartridges` at 2560. `automatic_actions` follow at 2624 and smokeless powder at 2627.
- **Artillery:** rifled breech-loaders come at 2559, the recoil-absorbing field gun at 2659 and `indirect_fire` at 2664.
- **Fleets:** `armored_hulls` come at 2557, `submersible_hulls` at 2667 and all-big-gun battleships at 2683. `carrier_aviation` follows at 2712 and `nuclear_propulsion` at 2810.
- **Industrial war:** continuous trenches come at 2704 and `armored_vehicles` at 2709. Radar arrives at 2764 and the armoured division at 2768.
- **Mass destruction:** the fission weapon comes at 2787, the thermonuclear weapon at 2805 and intercontinental missiles at 2818.
- **Information age:** satellite-guided strike comes at 2902, cyber commands at 2945 and small drones at 2980. The last 30 game years hold near-future items, marked speculative-plausible.

**Research time.** Time is given in game years while a staffed Security team is working on the item. Real minutes are for **1 day/s**: 1 game year is about 6 minutes, and 10 game years are about 1 hour. At 3 days/s, divide by 3.

**Id column.** A plain `id` is in the main catalog today; its year comes from `HISTORICAL_YEAR` through the CURVE unless the last table gives a correction. `NEW` = not authored yet. No `NEW` slug repeats an id in `docs/research/registry.json` (0–600), `docs/research/y600/registry_1200.json` (600–1200), `docs/research/y1200/registry_1800.json` (1200–1800), `scripts/*.gd` or another 2400–3000 list. "(continues: id)" names an earlier item that the row improves. "(shared: X)" marks an item that straddles into line X. `[gov: …]` marks a row that changes government, civic life or the court. Bands are ±40 years, ±30 for key thresholds (bold), capped at 3000.

**"Today" column.** The earliest year seen in recorded headless campaigns (`docs/technology-review/pacing/*.json`). It is `n/s` when the item is in main but was not reached in any recorded run, and `—` when it is not in main.

The total is **130** advances: 57 in years 2400–2700 and 73 in years 2700–3000. 27 are catalog ids. Across the four channels, one lands about every 4–5 game years. Most items take 5–10 years (30–61 min). Key thresholds (bold) take 8–15 years. The fission weapon takes 20 years (2 h), because it needs a state-scale program. Years 2700–2850 are densest: they hold the two world-scale industrial wars and the first nuclear decades (AD 1912–1970).

**Key thresholds:**
1. **Infantry firepower:** percussion caps (2453) → revolvers (2497) → needle rifle (2509) → expanding-base bullet (2531) → `range_estimation_drill` musketry school (2541) → `metallic_cartridges` (2560) → repeating rifles (2567) → volley guns (2576) → `automatic_actions` (2624) → smokeless powder (2627) → machine-gun companies (2685). Generals gain aimed fire at long range, then fire from lying down. Close order and the massed charge stop working.
2. **Artillery:** case shot (2416) → rifled breech-loaders (2559) → high explosive (2579) → recoil-absorbing gun (2659) → `indirect_fire` (2664) → field telephones (2677) → sound and flash ranging (2711). Generals can shell a target they cannot see.
3. **Command and organization:** all-arms divisions (1800–2400 `all_arms_divisions`, 2360) → army corps (2405) → class-year conscription (2411) → war college (2427; continues `military_staffs`) → field telegraph (2544) → railway mobilization (2557) → reservist mobilization (2587) → examined commissions (2589) → naval war college (2625) → sea-power doctrine (2640) → radio command nets (2747) → defence ministry (2792) → data links (2888) → cyber command (2945). Generals command larger armies, farther away and faster, and they argue from staff studies in conversation.
4. **The industrial battlefield:** barbed wire (2665) → trenches (2704) → war gases (2708) → `armored_vehicles` (2709) → infiltration squads (2713) → `infantry_tank_cooperation` (2714) → armoured division (2768) → `airborne_operations` (2775) → `infantry_antitank_coordination` (2776) → landing craft (2781) → helicopter assault (2839) → night vision (2842) → small drones (2980).
