# Autonomous cannery investment

The ordinary monthly civilian controller can now commission a cannery or request missing manufacturing inputs. Power and scientific-instrument recommendations retain their existing priority; cannery planning runs before generic civilian replenishment. Orders use the existing production and installation submission paths, spending actual materials and commissioning labor.

Investment requires a settled primary site, positive workforce condition and locally adopted thermal process validation, food retorts and double seaming. Hunger and war continue to suppress civilian investment at the controller. Any enabled plant under construction defers another project. A paused cannery is respected. Existing operators, two new cannery operators and one worker for commissioning/remaining work must fit the assigned Crafting workforce; the planner does not allocate population itself.

Visible perishable stocks must cover a nominal thirty days of capacity for all installed canneries plus one, while the overall food pool retains three days of current demand. Current estimated perishable spoilage must exceed the corresponding processing loss. That estimate uses the food system's base spoilage rates, storage pits, discovered preservation modifiers and current-day refrigeration service. It does not assume future harvests or read another civilization's hidden stocks.

Every operating input must either already cover thirty nominal days for the resulting installation count or have a feasible ordinary manufacturing recommendation. A raw coal/water shortage blocks investment even if cans could be manufactured. Missing can sets are requested through the recursive supply planner; missing retorts or sealing machinery similarly use normal manufacturing before installation. The planner returns one action per decision and never grants inputs, equipment, workers or plants. Once available surplus supports existing capacity only, additional installation stops.

The stock horizon and spoilage comparison are provisional investment heuristics. They are not a thirty-day spoilage forecast, proof of profitability, transport-demand forecast or guarantee of future supply. Current cold-storage service may change with fuel and labor availability. Daily operation now uses the same shared available-input calculation to scale supplies and workers to current perishable surplus, without changing the user's enabled/paused setting. This is a current-stock review, not scheduling of future harvest batches. General operating-input replenishment handles an installed plant; diplomatic acquisition of unavailable raw coal or water remains outside this planner.

No save fields changed. The helper reads existing owner-scoped food, knowledge, workforce, inventory and plant records. Focused tests cover actual controller payment and commissioning, suppression of duplicate/unsupported expansion, can and retort manufacturing requests, raw-supply blockers, spoilage/refrigeration, food reserve and workforce/adoption checks, and pause/scope/emergency guards. Physical canning and saved plant/food/input continuity are covered by the canning-preservation suite. Full-campaign economic benefit remains unverified.
