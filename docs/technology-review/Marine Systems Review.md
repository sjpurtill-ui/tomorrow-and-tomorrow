The original broader ship propulsion, stability and navigation entries are not counted a second time; these rows identify specific additional mechanisms and hardware.

Waterjets accelerate flow through a pump/nozzle, azimuth units rotate their thrust axis, tunnel thrusters create transverse flow, annular ducts alter loaded-propeller thrust and rotating cap fins address hub-flow losses. None supplies free thrust or a generic efficiency multiplier. Measured installation curves must include intake, cavitation, clearance, drag and shaft-power constraints.

Conventional active fins use forward-flow lift. Passive liquid tanks require appropriate phase and can impose free-surface penalties. Gyros consume spin-up/maintenance energy and have finite angular momentum and precession travel. Bilge plates dissipate roll through hydrodynamic response. A bulbous bow can worsen resistance outside its intended draft or speed, while air lubrication must earn its net benefit after compressor demand.

Stern-tube air barriers need actual pressure and leakage handling. Shaft earthing supplies a sliding current path but does not install a separate cathodic protection system. Centrifugal bilge treatment retains oil, sludge and rejected liquid; it does not create drinking water or grant regulatory discharge permission. A gyrocompass determines heading, while a Doppler log reports velocity relative to a specified acoustic reference; neither reveals exact hidden world state.
