# Prepared charcoal and coal-independent ironmaking

Charcoal Production now supplies a physical Charcoal stock. Two authored discoveries add controlled bloomery charges and retort production. Five finite recipes connect the fuel to existing ironworking instead of treating every furnace fuel supply as raw timber.

| Recipe | Materials per output batch | Output | Craft work days | Paid line tooling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mound-fired charcoal | 4 Timber | 1 Charcoal | 3 | 2 Clay, 2 Stone |
| Retort charcoal | 3 Timber | 1 Charcoal | 2 | 2 Pressure Vessels, 6 Refractory Bricks, 2 Wrought Iron |
| Measured bloomery charge | 3 Iron Ore, 1 Charcoal | 1 Wrought Iron | 3 | 18 Stone, 12 Clay |
| Charcoal blast furnace | 3 Iron Ore, 1 Charcoal, 0.3 Limestone | 1 Pig Iron | 3 | 12 Refractory Bricks, 4 Wrought Iron, 4 Timber |
| Charcoal finery | 1.2 Pig Iron, 0.5 Charcoal | 1 Wrought Iron | 2 | 8 Stone, 6 Clay, 2 Wrought Iron |

Retort research requires charcoal, pressure vessels and refractory brick firing together. Bloomery Charge Control requires bloomery smelting and standard measures together. Both have explicit local routes, no calendar gate and no global effect bonus. Finery and blast-furnace recipes use their existing research identities. Existing timber recipes remain bundled wood-fuel preparation alternatives. Separately prepared charcoal permits different production organization and stock acquisition; it does not grant a universal cost or speed advantage.

The blast-furnace graph now has a genuine charcoal route: rope rigging remains common, while charcoal, refractory brick firing and bloomery smelting supply a third explicit approach. The two previous approaches retain their refractory-furnace prerequisite within each route. Ore and flux evidence remain required. Ten actual Charcoal batches can satisfy the experimental fuel basis instead of coal access or ten Coal batches. This stock alternative is disclosed in the technology tree, contributes ordinary stock evidence, and suppresses an unnecessary experimental coal consignment. Research still does not consume experimental stocks, consistent with the existing material-evidence mechanism; operating recipes consume actual fuel separately.

The physical distinction and labor burden of charcoal-fired ironmaking are documented by the [National Park Service](https://home.nps.gov/articles/000/iron-making-smelting.htm). The [FAO discussion of carbonization](https://www.fao.org/4/X5555E/x5555e03.htm) explains how conversion yield and equipment affect wood requirements. Game quantities and the selected retort advantage are provisional balance choices, not measured historical yields or claims that every retort outperforms every kiln. Pressure Vessels abstracts an enclosed fabrication capability here, not operation of a pressurized pyrolysis reactor.

No thermal simulation, wood moisture/quality, byproduct recovery, emissions, continuous-furnace staffing, charcoal transport losses or furnace wear is added. There is no special charcoal investment AI; existing recursive production planning can evaluate the recipes. Long-campaign adoption and regional economics remain unverified.

No new save schema or stock grants. The normal generic production/stock records store charcoal and queues. Existing furnace research remains valid. The common resource predicate gains optional authored alternative stocks; eligibility, evidence scoring and missing-material explanations use the same alternative. Materials diplomacy still defaults to the existing coal consignment if neither fuel basis is available.
