# Military specimen research

Seventeen explicit military equipment examples can support destructive examination through the existing research atlas. Civilian examples remain available. An example must exist in the current civilization's unassigned, serviceable military inventory. Assigned formation equipment, damaged gear, foreign holdings and unfinished production cannot pay for examination.

| Bow | Bow craft |

These are explicit game mappings, not a claim that taking an object apart teaches every associated capability. Organizational and tactical gates are excluded. The investigation still needs its causal foundations, apprenticeship and workshop standards. One example is consumed immediately; researchers then contribute 180 study-work before receiving the existing 1.35× evidence multiplier. Learning and adoption follow ordinary research. A stronger existing study or repeated assignment prevents another payment. Knowledge is never granted by the consumption itself.

Naval and air examples use one equipment unit in the existing abstract inventory, not a separately simulated hull, engine or airframe. Detailed component salvage, engineering-grade sample quality and variable examination difficulty remain absent. This extension does not itself procure or capture equipment, establish licenses, or provide foreign operating services. Generals retain battlefield control.

Save records retain the existing reverse-engineered collection schema. The specimen ID uses a military: prefix and must match the explicit subject map; altered subject/item contracts are rejected. Existing civilian specimen saves retain their identifiers. Older versions cannot validate the new military examples.
