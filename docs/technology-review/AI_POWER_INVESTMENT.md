# AI generation investment

Monthly civilian decisions can commission a generator when existing enabled powered manufacturing or installed machinery has more demand than the enabled generation fleet can supply at current health and labor condition. This runs within the existing AI-controller guard. The ordinary `plant_install` civilization order calls TechnologyOperations.install: it consumes stored components immediately and adds commissioning work; it does not create installed capacity or electricity immediately.

The planner prefers an affordable solar array, then an affordable steam generator. When neither is immediately affordable, it can request ordinary manufacturing of missing components through CivilianProductionPlanner. Those orders still require adopted methods or valid licenses, actual inputs, tooling, workers and a free/reusable workshop. It does not overwrite active lines to obtain capacity.

Expansion is one unit per decision and waits while enabled machinery is already being commissioned. Disabled installations remain disabled. Known/adopted plant foundations, a settled primary home, available Crafting capacity with one worker left for other production, and a month of each fuel/input for steam are required. Current reservations are added back before comparing total effective Crafting capacity with the fleet's operator requirement, avoiding double-counting already assigned operators. Existing adequate nominal capacity prevents additional construction even if today's electricity has been consumed. Hunger and war suppress investment at the controller level.

Scope and limitations: demand comes from existing supplied, unpaused, below-target powered lines and installed machinery. It does not yet select a first powered process from an unstarted research-production plan, automatically construct consumer plants, procure missing raw fuels, model future electricity/storage/weather, or solve a one-slot workshop blocked by its own active powered line. Nominal adequate capacity with a fuel shortage calls for replenishment, not another generator; that replenishment policy remains separate. Missing components that themselves need unavailable electricity cannot bootstrap a solar chain; an affordable nonpowered steam route can still be considered. These limitations remain on the full-overhaul work list.

No new population, save or electricity ledger is introduced. Imported-service contracts and broader AI infrastructure planning remain unfinished.
