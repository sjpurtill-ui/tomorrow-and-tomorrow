# AI generation investment

Monthly civilian decisions can commission a generator when existing enabled powered manufacturing, a proposed first powered supply line, or installed machinery has more demand than the enabled generation fleet can supply at current health and labor condition. This runs within the existing AI-controller guard. The ordinary `plant_install` civilization order calls TechnologyOperations.install: it consumes stored components immediately and adds commissioning work; it does not create installed capacity or electricity immediately.

The planner prefers an affordable solar array, then an affordable steam generator. When neither is immediately affordable, it can request ordinary manufacturing of missing components through CivilianProductionPlanner. Those orders still require adopted methods or valid licenses, actual inputs, tooling, workers and a free/reusable workshop. It does not overwrite active lines to obtain capacity.

Expansion is one unit per decision and waits while enabled machinery is already being commissioned. Disabled installations remain disabled. Known/adopted plant foundations, a settled primary home, available Crafting capacity with one worker left for other production, and a month of each fuel/input for steam are required. Current reservations are added back before comparing total effective Crafting capacity with the fleet's operator requirement, avoiding double-counting already assigned operators. Existing adequate nominal generation capacity prevents redundant generator construction even if today's electricity has been consumed. Hunger and war suppress investment at the controller level.

First-industry planning: CivilianProductionPlanner can compare powered recipes even before electricity exists, while still checking knowledge, physical inputs and tooling. The controller passes the proposed line’s demand to the investment planner. Once generation is commissioned, a separate capacity check uses current fuel, health and total effective operators rather than the zero dispatch that is expected before the first line exists. The line then creates ordinary demand on subsequent days. Default supply calls still require existing power, including generation-component planning, which prevents circular free-power assumptions. An unavailable investment retains an affordable unpowered alternative. A completed pulp line can be retooled through the existing paid path into paper production using one workshop slot.

Scope and limitations: proposed demand follows the current paper/printed-study supply objective. It does not automatically construct consumer plants, procure missing raw fuels, model future weather or lifetime battery wear, or solve a one-slot workshop already blocked by its own active powered line. Nominal adequate capacity with a fuel shortage calls for replenishment, not another generator; that replenishment policy remains separate. Missing components that themselves need unavailable electricity cannot bootstrap a solar chain; an affordable nonpowered steam route can still be considered. These limitations remain on the full-overhaul work list.

No new population, save or electricity ledger is introduced. Imported-service contracts and broader AI infrastructure planning remain unfinished.

The recipe comparison uses workshop work, not a full lifetime investment cost. Commissioning, fuel and tooling are still paid, but their long-term value is not optimized. Tests exercise daily decision calls to verify the dependency sequence; the actual controller retains its monthly review schedule. No campaign-scale timing conclusion follows from that test.


Battery reserves: after commissioned generation exceeds current demand, monthly investment can buy a supervised or regulated bank, preferring an affordable regulated bank. It targets approximately three days of current demand, bounded by aggregate bank discharge rates and usable capacity. This is a planning target, not a promise that staffing, future demand or future fuel will cooperate. Existing banks count toward the target even when empty, preventing repeated purchases merely because charging has not finished. Enabled commissioning blocks another investment. Current generator fuel must cover a nominal month; disabled banks remain disabled. Missing bank components use the ordinary supply planner and paid workshops.

The first-line capacity check can use actual stored energy after next-day idle retention, bounded by bank discharge rates, health and remaining operators. It reserves a worker for manufacture and never changes balances during a quote. Empty, disabled, uncommissioned or unstaffed storage grants no capacity. Storage is not counted as permanent generation for expansion planning. Tests cover controller purchase, commissioning, charging, fuel interruption, reserve caps, insufficient fuel/staff, paused banks, upstream manufacture, control adoption and read-only finite-energy eligibility.
