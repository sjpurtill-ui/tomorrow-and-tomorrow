# Intaglio plates and impressions

Six authored discoveries add a recessed-image route to Printed Sheets: Copperplate Preparation, Burin Engraving, Drypoint Printmaking, Mezzotint Printmaking, Steelplate Engraving and Rolling Intaglio Printing. The rolling press requires paper, oil printing ink and bearings, plus any of the four image-making methods. It does not require a relief-printing invention. Common foundations still apply to foreign-study routes.

Prepared copper feeds three alternative plate methods. Steel engraving uses steel directly after its engraving and refining foundations. Nine ordinary civilian recipes manufacture the plates and impressions. Intaglio stock cannot substitute for relief Printing Forms, or vice versa. Staff, paid workshop tooling, available stocks and production targets remain mandatory. Licenses and destructive examination discover these recipes through the existing civilian recipe registry.

| Plate route | Plate-making work | Plate stock consumed per impression |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Burin-engraved copper | 6 | 0.02 |
| Drypoint copper | 4 | 0.10 |
| Mezzotint copper | 8 | 0.04 |
| Engraved steel | 9 | 0.01 |

Copper preparation adds three work and consumes one Refined Copper. Each impression costs two work, one Paper, 0.12 Printing Ink, 0.2 Freshwater and the listed plate wear. Press tooling consumes Timber, Wrought Iron, Shaft Bearings and Woven Cloth. Plate stocks represent remaining usable capacity in aggregate; consumed wear is not returned as scrap. Finished Printed Sheets use the existing finite study-support mechanism, with no extra research multiplier or automatic discovery.

The [V&A printmaking reference](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/what-is-print) distinguishes recessed printing from relief, describes burin cutting, retained drypoint burrs, mezzotint rocking and burnishing, and printing under pressure onto damp paper. It also discusses the limited useful impressions of printing surfaces. The exact work and wear coefficients above are game balance choices, not historical edition sizes or engineering measurements.

Limitations: plate batches do not retain image content, artist identity, tonal fidelity, individual damage, repair or export provenance. Mezzotint currently supplies the same study utility despite its greater preparation cost; its tonal representational advantage is described but not simulated. Engraved steel offers a copper-independent stock route and low wear; drypoint offers cheaper setup with higher recurring plate demand. No lithography, etching chemistry, censorship, book distribution or general cultural-production model is claimed here.
