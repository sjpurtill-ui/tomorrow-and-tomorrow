# Coke and indirect ironworking

Coke firing and continuous blast furnaces now produce physical stocks through ordinary finite workshop lines. A new Finery Forges discovery converts pig iron into the existing Wrought Iron supply used by equipment and industry. The original bloomery recipe remains available. This adds one authored identity, not duplicate coke or furnace discoveries.

| Method | Consumed per batch | Output | Craft work days | One-time line tooling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Coke Firing | 2 Coal | 1 Coke | 3 | 8 Stone, 6 Clay |
| Continuous Blast Furnace | 3 Iron Ore, 1 Coke, 0.3 Limestone | 1 Pig Iron | 2 | 12 Refractory Bricks, 4 Wrought Iron, 4 Timber |
| Finery Forges | 1.2 Pig Iron, 2 Timber | 1 Wrought Iron | 2 | 8 Stone, 6 Clay, 2 Wrought Iron |

Values represent balanced game batches, not physical tonnes or measured historical yields. Timber represents wood-derived furnace fuel, as in existing bloomery production; separate charcoal manufacture and fuel quality are still absent. Coke assumes suitable graded coal; volatile byproducts, sulfur contamination, emissions, continuous furnace staffing and refractory wear are not simulated here. The blast furnace remains a workshop recipe rather than a separately commissioned operating plant.

Finery research requires blast-furnace knowledge and forge welding together, has an explicit local learning route and no calendar gate. Existing blast-furnace research retains its mining-supported and metallurgical alternatives, experimental materials, refractory practice and lifting prerequisites. The recipes consume real intermediate stocks regardless of how those were acquired. Coke firing and blast-furnace discovery no longer award their former global fuel, yield, construction, quality or pollution modifiers; their new benefit requires production. Other discoveries retain their existing effects.

The indirect route reconverges on existing wrought-iron uses. It reduces recurring timber demand at the cost of coal preparation, additional work, refining losses and larger tooling investment; it is not an unconditional speed upgrade. Existing production planning can evaluate these alternative recipes using normal material, knowledge and labor checks. No special AI preference or campaign-scale superiority is claimed.

The historical distinction between coke-fueled iron smelting and later refining is supported by the [Ironbridge Gorge Museum Trust](https://www.ironbridge.org.uk/our-story/the-iron-bridge/) and the [National Park Service explanation of fining and hammering](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/iron-making-refining-into-wrought-iron.htm). These sources support the processes, not the game quantities.

No save schema changes. Existing known methods gain recipe access according to normal adoption/license rules; no stock is granted retroactively. New stocks and queues use existing generic save records. Removing the old discovery modifiers changes balance in old campaigns and requires integration review.

Focused tests exercise the real production line chain, exact fuel/ore/tooling and refining-loss accounting, absent-input blockers, preserved bloomery access, authored prerequisites and removal of unpaid bonuses. Structural recipe reachability is not evidence of a functioning full-history campaign.
