# Digital logic and hardwired sequencing

Eight authored discoveries extend the electronic-component chain to digital control. They have no calendar gates. This implementation covers aggregate switching, state-storage and arithmetic assemblies; general-purpose computers remain unfinished.

| Discovery | Causal foundations | Physical result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Relay Logic | Electromagnetic Relays | Logic Modules from relays and wire |
| Diode Logic | Silicon Rectifiers, Carbon Resistors | Logic Modules from diodes, resistors and wire |
| Transistor Inverters | Bipolar Junction Transistors, Carbon Resistors | Inverter Modules |
| Bistable Multivibrators | Transistor Inverters | Bistable Modules |
| Binary Counters | Bistable Multivibrators | Counter Modules |
| Shift Registers | Bistable Multivibrators | Register Modules |
| Binary Adders | Relay Logic **or** Diode Logic | Adder Modules through relay or semiconductor assembly recipes |
| Hardwired Sequence Control | Binary Counters, Shift Registers, Binary Adders, Electronic Machine Control | Sequence Controllers |

The Computer History Museum documents relay-based demonstration adders and binary relay machines in its [computer timeline](https://www.computerhistory.org/timeline/computers/), and transistor storage applications in its [storage history](https://www.computerhistory.org/storageengine/transistors-make-fast-memories/). These support the distinct switching/arithmetic/storage mechanisms. They do not establish the game's batch sizes, prerequisite choices or production coefficients. No monolithic integrated circuit is represented by these discrete assemblies.

Nine recipes consume finite stocks and workshop labor, including two arithmetic implementations. Relay arithmetic can be produced without silicon components. Diode logic does not provide inversion by itself; semiconductor arithmetic consumes separately manufactured transistor inverters. Storage assemblies consume two inverter batches; counters and shift registers consume bistable assemblies. Final controllers consume one counter, register, adder and earlier electronic controller plus insulated cable. Every line pays tooling. Existing production licenses and destructive specimen study discover civilian recipes through their normal dynamic registries; neither grants free knowledge or components.

A sixth plant, **Hardwired sequencing workshop**, costs one Sequence Controller, one Electric Motor, two Insulated Cable and three Steel. It commissions through 18 worker-days, then requires 1.5 Crafting operators and three daily electricity units for five mechanical-work service units at full operating condition. It shares the existing workshop workforce multiplier and its cap. Stocks alone provide no service; loss of operators or electricity stops operation. Existing motor and analog-controller workshops remain alternatives. Installation knowledge/adoption requirements remain distinct from licensed component manufacture.

Save validation now permits the six-type maximum of 12,000 simultaneous commissioning workers and 12,500 mechanical-work service units. These are bounded aggregate maxima, not a claim that a normal city can staff that many plants. No new state fields are introduced; old builds cannot operate new discovery, recipe or plant IDs or the expanded bounds.

Limitations: no per-bit execution, clock waveforms, propagation delays, fan-out, contact wear, noise margins, program memory, software or task-specific machine sequences. Logic Modules abstract compatible assemblies and do not preserve manufacturing provenance; a mixed-source stock is treated as interchangeable. Batch counts are game units, not exact transistor/gate counts. Service coefficients are balance choices, not measured industrial productivity. Research branches reconverge; the relay route is an independent arithmetic route, but the new sequencing endpoint still needs transistor-based storage and earlier electronic control. Research acquisition, geography, adoption and material shortages can slow these paths without an era lock.

Validation and catalog counts are recorded in IMPLEMENTATION_STATUS.md. Native player integration and full-history pacing remain unverified.
