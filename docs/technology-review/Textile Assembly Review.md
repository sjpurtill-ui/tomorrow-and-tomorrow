# Textile assembly coverage

Twelve authored proposals extend D07 to its 120 planned identities; this is allocation coverage, not runtime completion. Each mechanism has a distinct material or assembly operation and an actual proposed consumer. None grants a global warmth, strength, waterproofing or safety bonus.

Braiding interlaces moving yarn paths rather than twisting cord. Tufting inserts yarn into a backing rather than weaving a pile. Air covering combines a purchased elastomeric core and filaments without inventing the elastomer. Quilting restrains filling whose actual thermal behavior remains separate. A reinforced button opening, interlocking zipper chain, hook/loop interface and resilient snap socket are distinct closure mechanisms with different parts and failure behavior. Seam taping must seal real needle paths in a compatible garment. Skiving removes thickness locally, while finishing applies a tested surface coating to already tanned leather. Needle lace builds a freestanding thread mesh rather than embroidering a permanent ground.

Every row requires paid materials, work, tools and acceptance before its proposed consumer can use the resulting component. Imported compatible components permit assembly under known methods without granting their manufacture. Five paid external-learning contracts remain required. Partial jobs, local stock, actual garment state and save continuation must be implemented before runtime promotion.

Sources are original manufacturer explanations, a published leather-coating patent, official textile process documentation and museum collection expertise. The coating patent supports a specific flexible binder/pigment mechanism rather than every surface finish. Prerequisites and game costs are design choices. Historical horizons classify chosen manual or industrial scope, not exclusive invention dates. No numerical industrial operating recipe is supplied.
