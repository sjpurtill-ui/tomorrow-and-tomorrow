# Governance, economy and learning

180 authored drafts: 60 in each field. These are proposed institutional or technical capabilities, not enacted policies or implemented bonuses. Communities can learn competing institutional methods; choosing operating rules belongs to civic decisions. Governance, labor and population remain with their existing simulation owners.

All shared foundations are required. One member of each alternative group suffices. Actual staff, equipment, resources, authority and enforcement are separately required. Foreign instruction and research acquisition must retain local work and disadvantages; the generic recovery notes are not completed route implementations.

## Sources and review limits

The procurement entries distinguish purchasing procedures, workforce, controls and accountability, consistent with the OECD’s discussion of [integrity in public procurement](https://www.oecd.org/en/topics/integrity-in-public-procurement.html). These are proposed game mechanisms, not a claim that a named institution eliminates corruption.

Payment and clearing entries distinguish liquidity, settlement obligations and finality; see the BIS [Principles for Financial Market Infrastructures](https://www.bis.org/committees/cpmi/pfmi/overview). The distant-settlement and long-duration finance entries extrapolate game requirements and are not descriptions of established interplanetary markets.

Shared equipment, repositories, training and research infrastructure are distinct capacities in UNESCO’s [Recommendation on Open Science](https://www.unesco.org/en/legal-affairs/recommendation-open-science). This supports separating publication from the practical ability to reproduce and operate a method.

These overview sources do not validate every historical prerequisite. Individual historical review, semantic duplicate reconciliation, broader empirical alternatives, explicit recovery routes and implemented operating effects remain outstanding.
