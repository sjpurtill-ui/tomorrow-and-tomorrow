# Medicine and Care Depth Review

This section adds 60 authored D14 proposals covering physical examination, diagnostics, pharmaceutical processes, respiratory and nutrition support, maternal care, dentistry and rehabilitation. At this publication checkpoint the complete master accounts for **2,101 identities: 556 implemented baseline, 48 pending communications and 1,497 master drafts**. **2,899 remain to author.** D14 now accounts for 133 of its 280 editorial target, leaving 147. None of these new proposals is a deployed clinical operating system.

## Distinct mechanisms and useful choices

The proposals distinguish a measured signal, its interpretation, an actual product and a staffed care service. Knowing how to record an ECG does not create a machine or supply a clinician. Producing insulin does not treat a person; qualified supply, monitoring, delivery and continuing care remain separate. Rehabilitation requires fitted equipment, suitable surroundings and practiced human support, rather than a flat population-productivity bonus.

The scope reconciliation file records five broad earlier discoveries and their distinct specialized mechanisms. Clinical Chemistry remains the general measurement and interpretation framework; it does not count as already knowing every enzyme assay, ion-selective method or gas analyzer. Intensive Care remains service coordination rather than ownership of every organ-support machine. Earlier IDs and data are preserved; reviewers must confirm these distinctions before runtime integration.

There are fifteen alternative foundation groups. Empirical examination, calibrated acoustic assessment, non-digital control and practical fitting can precede later explanations and instruments. These alternatives grant only the capability supported by the actual method and resources. No era/date gates or mandatory political forms are introduced.

## Imported research and care remain disadvantaged

Each JSON record specifies five proposed recovery methods. A paid scholar needs a competent source, real travel, board and finite supervised teaching. A partnership requires reciprocal contributions, access and local validation. Purchased research commissions a defined question and pays for delivery; negative findings remain possible. Licensed products or external care consume continuing payments and capacity, preserving supplier dependence. Reverse engineering requires a delivered object or usable process evidence and appropriate analysis; it cannot alone establish clinical benefit or practical competence.

For example, a society with qualified medicine handling and glucose assessment can develop insulin-replacement care using purchased qualified insulin. It still needs actual products, delivery supplies and trained caregivers. Supply loss exhausts stocks and interrupts dependent care; it does not magically produce domestic purification knowledge. A later domestic production chain can reduce that dependence after paying for its own facilities, materials and qualification.

A society can assess hearing with an evaluated acoustic reference method before electronic audiometry. Its range and precision remain those actually supported by the equipment and practitioner. Importing a more advanced instrument adds maintenance, calibration and training obligations; it does not instantly supply interpretation skill.

Runtime work coefficients, supply-chain recipes, clinical labor allocation, finite care throughput, outcome models, licenses and AI investment are still pending. The design does not add a competing population or labor authority: existing aggregate people and GovernmentPeopleSystem responsibilities remain authoritative.

## Evidence grounding

WHO describes access to diagnostics as a health-system problem spanning quality, devices and the access chain. This supports separating an assay mechanism from a staffed, supplied diagnostic service. [WHO diagnostics](https://www.who.int/health-topics/diagnostics).

WHO treats rehabilitation as part of health systems, including workforce, assistive technology and service delivery. Its assistive-technology work includes procurement, distribution and provision; an equipment unlock alone is therefore an inadequate game model. [WHO rehabilitation](https://www.who.int/health-topics/rehabilitation/integrating-rehabilitation-into-health-systems), [WHO assistive technology](https://www.who.int/teams/noncommunicable-diseases/sensory-functions-disability-and-rehabilitation/rehabilitation/assistive-technology).

NIDDK describes insulin's development as building on prior research and distinguishes the sensor, pump and integrated control work behind automated diabetes-management devices. This section uses that separation for product purification, monitored treatment, sensing and delivery integration. [NIDDK insulin development](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/news/archive/2021/celebrating-discovery-development-insulin), [NIDDK automated delivery research](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/news/archive/2017/story-discovery-artificial-pancreas-managing-type1-diabetes).

These sources ground selected distinctions. They do not validate all prerequisite edges, first-invention dates, clinical performance or game balance. Full historical-horizon allocation and wider technical review remain outstanding.
