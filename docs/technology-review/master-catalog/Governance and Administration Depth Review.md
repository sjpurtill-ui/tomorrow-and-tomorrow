# Governance and Administration Depth Review

This section adds 54 D16 proposals after excluding six applications already covered by existing procedures. It deepens succession, representation, public deliberation, civic records, tax assessment and fiscal administration. At this publication checkpoint the catalog accounts for **2,331 identities: 556 implemented baseline, 48 pending communications and 1,727 master drafts**. **2,669 remain to author.** Governance accounts for 120 of its 240 editorial target, leaving 120.

These are learnable methods, not enacted policies. Every record explicitly separates learning from adoption. Discovering a voting method does not change the franchise, appoint officials or create a legislature. Discovering a tax method does not collect revenue. Actual authority, participants, staff, records and resources remain operating requirements.

## Competing paths and consequences

Hereditary and council-elected succession are different methods for ordering claims to office. Neither is a universal predecessor of the other or of scientific progress. Different vote-counting systems translate preferences into outcomes through different rules; none guarantees legitimacy, expertise or consensus. Tax methods expose distinct bases and collection timing, with real unequal burdens, compliance costs and behavioral effects.

The proposed Public Decision Witnessing, Succession Dispute Adjudication, Assessment Appeal Tribunals, Service Eligibility Appeals, Public Project Stage Reviews and Public Consultation Response Logs were excluded. Their mechanisms are already represented by witnessed obligations, evidentiary hearings, administrative appeals, staged project commitments or petition records. These functions remain available design requirements without new discovery counts.

General public levies remain a broad collection practice; fixed-person, income, product and invoice-credit bases are distinct calculation mechanisms. Census rolls enumerate populations periodically; continuous vital events and current residence report different observations. Scope reconciliation records these distinctions for implementation review.

## Outside learning and adoption

Every record proposes visiting instruction, reciprocal institutional research, a commissioned study, qualified outside assistance and document/procedure examination. Actual travel or record delivery, compensation, finite local learning and ongoing administrative capacity remain costs. A borrowed constitutional text is evidence for study, not legitimacy or an automatic institutional transformation.

A society may pay visiting scholars to explain a revenue-sharing formula and help local officials practice its calculation. The formula can only divide actually collected funds. A transfer appears once in combined accounts; it cannot create public resources in both the sending and receiving authority. Payments for outside assistance also consume real public resources.

A society can learn a proportional allocation method while continuing to use an existing council-selection rule. Applying it requires an explicitly adopted electorate, seat count, allocation rule and functioning count. The game must retain the actual chosen rules and their consequences rather than attach a universal representation bonus to the discovery.

GovernmentPeopleSystem remains the owner of civic officials and daily settlement labor. HistoricalFigures remains separate for exceptional contributors; these records create no duplicate officeholders, people or administrative simulation. Implementation must conserve aggregate population and treasury resources and keep ordinary administration within the existing governance flow.

The contracts remain proposals. Rule adoption, actual tax bases, fiscal transfers, service queues, political consequences, source availability, learning penalties and AI choices still need implementation and verification. Exclusion or coercion may be historical policy choices with real consequences, but are never mandatory prerequisites for unrelated advancement or free efficiency upgrades.

## Evidence grounding

International IDEA distinguishes plurality/majority, proportional and mixed systems and describes their different vote-to-seat mechanisms. This supports treating their procedures as alternatives with context-dependent outcomes rather than one linear superior progression. [International IDEA electoral systems](https://www.idea.int/data-tools/data/electoral-system-design-database).

OECD's fiscal-federalism work distinguishes taxation and spending powers across government levels and discusses both benefits and complications of decentralization. That supports separating assignment, revenue sharing, equalization and consolidated accounting instead of giving every additional government layer free resources. [OECD Fiscal Federalism](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/fiscal-federalism-2022_201c75b6-en/full-report.html).

The World Bank describes civil registration as recording vital events and distinguishes those records from their statistical use. That supports separate continuous event recording and population-enumeration mechanisms, with access and correction requirements. [World Bank civil registration](https://www.worldbank.org/en/olc/course/34651).

These sources support selected distinctions, not every historical first occurrence, prerequisite or political outcome. This is game-system design, not jurisdiction-specific legal or tax guidance. Historical-horizon allocation and broader semantic review remain outstanding.
