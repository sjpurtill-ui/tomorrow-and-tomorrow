# Mathematics, records and culture

179 authored drafts: 60 mathematics and measurement, 60 records and communication, and 59 culture discoveries. This section develops reasoning, transmission and creative practices alongside physical media and services. It is a review catalog, not an implemented gameplay delivery.

The section adds 22 alternative prerequisite groups. All shared foundations are required; one entry from each alternative group suffices. Empty shared foundations can describe a root practice, but actual people, evidence, materials and work remain necessary. No date, global era, language, religion or civilization identity is an unlock condition.

## What the discoveries make possible

Mathematical methods change which questions can be reasoned about, computed or measured. Their benefits are conditional. A sparse solver can reduce work for suitable systems; it is not a universal production bonus. A proof establishes its formal claim under its assumptions. A validated model has a demonstrated use domain, not access to unobserved ground truth.

Information systems create and maintain usable records, paths and interpretations. A message dispatched is not a message received. A stored file is not necessarily readable in the future. A verified digest can establish that bytes match a reference without proving the original account true. Actual copying, delivery, storage, translation and preservation consume resources.

Cultural practices create opportunities for expression, interpretation and participation. Their consequences depend on participants, access and local meaning. They do not automatically grant cohesion, legitimacy, universal happiness or a fixed national identity. The ruler can support conditions and resources; cultural life remains the activity of actual people.

## Recovery through other societies

Each record includes a proposed destination, local demonstration, operating requirements and costs of outside assistance. All acquisition routes reconverge on the same discovery identity. The coefficients and runtime mappings remain to be implemented and balanced.

A competent visiting scholar or practitioner can teach for payment or trade after an actual journey. A partnership requires reciprocal contributions and exchanged evidence. Delivered research arrives only through an existing contact and transport or communication route. Commissioned work can shorten elapsed research by purchasing capable outside labor, but requires payment, delivery and local interpretation. Rebuilding missing foundations remains possible and costs time.

A foreign proof, program, musical score or surviving artifact can provide evidence without granting local mastery. Learning requires the stated demonstration: checking the reasoning, operating the communication system, or practicing the cultural method. An imported service uses the supplier's real capacity and can disappear when access ends. Buying a work does not transfer every creative skill or establish rightful custodianship.

Cultural teaching requires willing practitioners and accountable access. Documentation can preserve evidence of a practice without keeping it alive. Continued language use depends on actual speakers, teachers and opportunities for transmission; it cannot be guaranteed by an archive technology.

Genealogical Cross-Checking focuses on independently checking claimed relationships; it is not another label for the general recitation method already present in Oral Epics. Iterative Game Playtesting concerns observed comparison and revision of rule variants, rather than duplicating the agreed contests already represented by Civic Games.

Rhythmic ensemble practice is a root method and does not require epic literature. Image making does not require a pictographic writing system. Instrument making can develop through skilled empirical work before formal experiments. Ensembles can rehearse by ear, and different instrument families support tuning or craft standards.

Geometric proof need not wait for algebra. Channel analysis need not wait for a physical sampling device. Bookbinding can use different writing surfaces. Reusable character composition can use wood or metal. Structured records can precede relational databases. Civilian protocols do not depend on military interoperability, and accessible records do not depend on accessible buildings.

Animation can use hand-authored sequences before photographic motion pictures. Film editing does not require textual collation. Language documentation can use audio without requiring an alphabet. Cultural collections need not begin as biological collections. These alternatives retain actual equipment, media and practice requirements.

## Selected grounding

NIST describes atomic clocks as instruments that reference measured atomic behavior. The proposed tree separates the timing source from comparisons and distribution across real links. [NIST, How Do Atomic Clocks Work?](https://www.nist.gov/atomic-clocks/how-do-atomic-clocks-work).

The Library of Congress maintains guidance on digital formats and their preservation. This supports distinguishing retained bits, interpretable formats, migration, emulation and continuing access. [Library of Congress, Digital Formats](https://www.loc.gov/programs/digital-collections-management/digital-formats/).

UNESCO describes oral traditions as living practices involving performance and transmission, and emphasizes the role of language in transmitting heritage. The proposed oral and audio routes therefore remain usable alongside writing. [UNESCO, Oral traditions and expressions](https://ich.unesco.org/en/oral-traditions-and-expressions-00053); [UNESCO, Living Heritage and mother languages](https://ich.unesco.org/en/ich-and-mother-languages-00555).
