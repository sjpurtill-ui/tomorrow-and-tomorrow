# Food and Agriculture Depth Review

This section adds 58 D01 proposals after excluding two overlapping process-control names. It expands food gathering and preparation, beekeeping, aquatic harvest, livestock care, food processing and biological food production. At this publication checkpoint the catalog accounts for **2,277 identities: 556 implemented baseline, 48 pending communications and 1,673 master drafts**. **2,723 remain to author.** Food and agriculture accounts for 161 of its 280 editorial target, leaving 119.

These proposals specify mechanisms and operating requirements; they do not add functioning farms, animals, process plants or food stocks to the game. Actual land, ecological productivity, feedstock, labor, water, energy, equipment and product qualification remain necessary.

## Early knowledge and distinct outputs

Edible Resource Recognition and Hearth Roasting Control are early learning roots. Recognizing food locally does not require a complete seasonal calendar, and cooking with fire does not require a pottery kiln. Their actual operating requirements remain explicit; unknown organisms are not assumed safe and knowledge does not provide fuel.

Pounding, dehulling, starch separation, cooking, chilling and packaging perform different physical transformations. Their outputs and losses must remain distinguishable when implemented. Grain Milling is not counted again under a synonym. The proposed Smoke Exposure Control and Fermentation Temperature Control entries were removed because the earlier Smoking and Fermentation Control identities already cover those practices.

Ice Chilling Foods consumes actual ice and transfers heat into melting water. Manufactured ice and suitable naturally available ice are alternative sources. It does not imply a refrigeration plant, unlimited cold or safe indefinite storage. Food-process descriptions are game design contracts, not household processing instructions.

## Disadvantaged outside learning

Each record proposes paid visiting instruction, reciprocal trials, commissioned research, licensed/imported supply and specimen or process examination. Sources must actually possess relevant knowledge or deliverable capacity. Travel, transport, local trials and continuing payments remain costs. A purchased crop variety, culture, animal, finished food or machine does not supply all necessary domestic knowledge.

For example, hired pollination requires compatible living colonies, forage, flowering crops, transport and an available provider. It can support suitable crops but is not a universal yield bonus. If a provider withdraws, the society loses that dependent service rather than retaining imaginary colonies. Learning local beekeeping requires suitable biological stocks and practiced husbandry.

A society can purchase processing service before constructing its own plant. The actual delivered food must come from real inputs and paid capacity. Examining a finished product may provide limited evidence; it does not reveal every processing step or demonstrate safety. Plant construction and local qualification are separate projects.

Feed and livestock technologies conserve actual animals and food. Better handling, care and nutrition may improve measured outcomes but do not create stock, guarantee births or erase disease. New biological food systems consume feedstock, energy and process supplies; product-specific qualification, nutritional properties and economics remain distinct. Producing an ingredient is not producing a complete diet.

The recorded recovery terms remain design proposals. Material flows, species compatibility, environmental carrying capacity, finite staffing, process yields, acquisition penalties and AI behavior still require implementation. GovernmentPeopleSystem remains the owner of existing daily labor and aggregate population; these drafts introduce no competing authority.

## Evidence grounding

FAO distinguishes managed beekeeping from honey gathering and connects pollinator services to agricultural production. This supports separate colony husbandry and crop-service contracts, with biological and ecological dependence rather than a universal crop multiplier. [FAO beekeeping](https://www.fao.org/4/y5110e/y5110e00.htm), [FAO pollination services](https://www.fao.org/pollination/).

FAO's post-harvest material treats losses across handling and processing stages, including changes in quantity and quality. The proposed loss measurements therefore track explicit boundaries rather than treating all mass change as edible food loss. [FAO post-harvest systems](https://www.fao.org/4/ac301e/AC301e03.htm).

FAO and WHO address food-safety considerations for cell-based production, and FAO maintains work on these processes and precision fermentation. The proposed cell expansion, structuring and ingredient-production capabilities retain product-specific qualification rather than automatically promising cheap, complete or universally suitable food. [FAO cell-based food production](https://www.fao.org/food-safety/scientific-advice/biotechnology--gmo-and-gm-foods/cell-based-food-and-precision-fermentation/cell-based-food-production-and-food-safety/).

These sources ground selected distinctions, not every proposed prerequisite, historical date, yield, safety outcome or scale-up claim. Further semantic and technical review and historical-horizon allocation remain necessary.
