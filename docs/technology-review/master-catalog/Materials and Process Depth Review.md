# Materials and Process Depth Review

This section adds 59 D04 proposals after excluding one repeated reuse-qualification mechanism. It deepens metal treatment and joining, polymer synthesis and forming, glass production and ceramic processing. At this publication checkpoint the catalog accounts for **2,390 identities: 556 implemented baseline, 48 pending communications and 1,786 master drafts**. **2,610 remain to author.** Materials accounts for 186 of its 300 editorial target, leaving 114.

These are authored process contracts, not functioning factories or new material stocks. Every usable output still requires suitable feedstock, plant, energy, staff, inspection and waste handling. Knowing a material's properties does not grant every method for producing it.

## Closing the polymer-synthesis gap

The earlier Synthetic Polymers candidate was only partially represented by chain models, shaping processes and elastomers. This section adds qualified monomer preparation and radical, ionic, coordination, condensative-step, additive-step and ring-opening synthesis mechanisms. The candidate is now mapped to authored identities; that does not claim complete industrial chemistry or operating implementation.

Chain and step polymerization are not treated as identical to the older blanket distinction between addition and condensation. Each record describes its actual reaction organization at a high level and retains qualified process-specific evidence. Some empirical synthesis and heat-treatment practices can precede a complete theoretical explanation.

Materials Service Requalification was removed because the existing Component Reuse Certification mechanism already addresses qualification for a new service. The separate reconciliation file preserves that exclusion and clarifies broad processing versus distinct forming mechanisms.

## Outside learning and physical consequences

Each record proposes paid visiting instruction, reciprocal specimens or research, commissioned studies, licensed/imported processing and actual specimen examination. Source availability, compensation, transport, local trials and ongoing supplies remain costs. A material sample can provide composition and property evidence without revealing its complete thermal history, tooling, process control or production scale.

A society can import qualified polymer resin and shape it locally before mastering its synthesis. The shaping plant still consumes actual resin, power, tooling and labor. Losing that supplier removes the feedstock stream after stored inventory is exhausted. Developing domestic synthesis requires its separate reaction route, feedstocks, plant and qualification; purchased resin does not silently grant those discoveries.

The same distinction applies to imported alloy stock, glass blanks and ceramic powders. Processing a component changes actual stock, produces losses and may alter properties. Strength, toughness, residual stress, fatigue resistance and thermal behavior are not one universal material-quality value. Operating qualification must be specific to the component and its duty.

Selective depolymerization is not universal perfect recycling. Suitable feedstock, recovery processes, purification, energy and residues remain part of the account. Graded and self-healing materials retain bounded performance claims; neither implies indefinite automatic repair.

Runtime recipes, property models, commissioned process capacity, material conservation, imported-service dependence, acquisition penalties and AI planning remain pending. No new processing inventory is claimed delivered by this document.

## Evidence grounding

IUPAC's polymerization classification distinguishes chain polymerization and additive or condensative step polymerization. The synthesis descriptions retain these mechanistic distinctions rather than assuming all additive reactions imply chain propagation. [IUPAC polymerization classification](https://iupac.org/recommendation/basic-classification-and-definitions-of-polymerization-reactions/).

NIST's material-qualification work distinguishes material properties, microstructure, heat treatment, densification, surface condition and inspection. This supports separate processing and qualification mechanisms rather than a single advanced-material bonus. [NIST additive part qualification](https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/additive-manufacturing-part-qualification).

DOE describes selective polymer deconstruction and subsequent separation as specific research pathways and emphasizes the complexity of mixed plastics. The recycling proposal therefore retains feedstock and recovery limits. [DOE polymer recycling research](https://www.energy.gov/science/bes/articles/new-polymers-close-loop-plastics-recycling), [DOE plastics circularity](https://www.energy.gov/science/articles/transforming-plastics-recycling-discovery-science).

These sources ground selected distinctions, not every prerequisite, historical first occurrence, process parameter, maturity assignment or numerical property. This document provides high-level game capability contracts, not industrial operating instructions. Wider technical and semantic review remains required.
