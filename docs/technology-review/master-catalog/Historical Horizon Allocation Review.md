# Historical Horizon Allocation Review

The 5,000-discovery target requires broad historical coverage as well as subject diversity. A field such as Human futures integration is not itself a historical horizon. Modern capabilities used in a future settlement remain modern mechanisms unless the authored identity specifies a distinct advanced capability.

This first explicit review assigns 94 existing identities: the 38 Energy Conversion and Storage Depth discoveries and 56 Human Futures Integration discoveries. It creates no new discoveries and changes no runtime prerequisites. The other 2,937 accounted identities remain **unassigned**, not absent from history. The catalog still accounts for 3,031 identities and has 1,969 identities left to author.

## What the review changes

Nine of the 94 reviewed identities are assigned to the advanced/future horizon. This is **not** a claim that the whole tree has only nine future discoveries; other sections have not yet received this review. It also does not establish that the eventual 1,300 allocation is satisfied.

The following distinctions prevent inflated future accounting:

- Modern prosthetic feedback, medical-record linkage and planetary-defense work remain modern even when useful to future civilizations. NASA's DART demonstrated a kinetic-impact orbital change; impact-deflection design cannot honestly be counted as wholly future technology. [NASA DART result](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-confirms-dart-mission-impact-changed-asteroids-motion-in-space/). NIH describes existing prosthetic movement-feedback research. [NIH prosthetic feedback](https://www.nih.gov/news-events/nih-research-matters/improving-control-bionic-prosthetic-hands).
- Ground-analog rotating-habitat studies and bounded life-support experiments remain modern research. The authored operating contract for rotating-habitat human factors expressly permits ground analogs. Longitudinal sustained partial-gravity health studies have a different frontier operating requirement. NASA's 2021 review described the lack of direct long-term human partial-gravity evidence; this supports the distinction, not a prediction of future health outcomes. [NASA technical review](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210019591).
- A research proposal's uncertain commercial performance does not automatically make it futuristic. Solid electrolytes, sodium intercalation and thermochemical water-splitting research are classified by their mechanisms, not marketing novelty.
- Fusion experiments and a sustained integrated fusion fuel cycle are different scopes. Divertor heat-envelope research remains modern here; plant-relevant breeding, recoverable fuel inventory and blanket heat extraction retain advanced assignments. These assignments do not assert that net power or self-supplied fuel has been achieved.
- Ordinary demographic projection, supply planning, mediation and custody of skills do not become new inventions merely by moving to a frontier location. Their existing identities retain their actual methodological scope.

## No calendar gates

An editorial horizon is neither an unlock date nor an exclusive real-world origin. Discoveries remain governed by causal prerequisites, physical opportunities, local research and disadvantaged outside-learning routes. An unusually capable civilization may develop an applicable mechanism earlier in the fictional calendar; a deprived civilization may lack it later. Imported equipment never changes a local knowledge identity's classification or grants mastery.

The viable 2,500–3,000-year game still requires operating research costs, opportunities, acquisition delays, recovery behavior and full-campaign evidence. This allocation does not certify that pacing. Exact Earth chronology is not fitted into the fictional calendar through category gates.
