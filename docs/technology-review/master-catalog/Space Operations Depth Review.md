# Space Operations Depth Review

57 additional D23 proposals after three overlap exclusions. These are authored designs, not implemented missions. The space field accounts for 117 of 180 planned identities.

The scope joins sky measurement to launch architecture, recovery, orbital servicing, cryogenic delivery and sustained off-world operations. Imported launch and refueling services remain possible without domestic mastery of every subsystem. Actual delivered hardware, qualified local interfaces, information delay, provider capacity, paid support and repeatable local demonstrations remain necessary.

## Recovery is not readiness

A recovered stage still needs inspection, consumed life accounting, replacement parts and paid turnaround. Propulsive, parachute and runway recovery remain alternatives. A surface lander can use condition-based requalification without first developing Earth-style booster recovery. An orbital transfer stage can remain in space; it does not need an atmospheric heat shield. Chemical refueling, electric-propulsion feed replenishment and exchanged finite motor modules each require their actual compatible supply and servicing chain.

The previous surface-power draft also required solar arrays universally. It now permits qualified fission-electric generation, with real thermal rejection, shielding, distribution and interruption reserves. A reactor is not a universal solution, and knowing the method does not install it.

## Branch review

- Angular Image Calibration: Refractive or reflective observation systems can supply a calibrated image; the selected instrument still needs adequate angular performance.
- Stellar Parallax Distance: Different optical architectures can measure the repeated displacement if their actual precision is sufficient.
- Variable-Star Distance Models: Calibrated repeated intensity observations may be photographic or instrumentally spectral; neither a raw image nor a spectrum alone supplies a distance.
- Radio Aperture Synthesis: Radio correlation and calibrated baseline reconstruction do not require prior optical interferometer construction.
- Coronagraphic Starlight Suppression: Rejection optics can be developed around either optical architecture; achievable contrast must be demonstrated.
- Parachute Stage Recovery: Recoverable stage hardware may carry solid or liquid propulsion; recovery does not establish that engines or propellant grains are reusable.
- Runway-Return Stage Integration: Replaceable ablative protection or qualified reusable surfaces can support repeated vehicle use with different refurbishment costs.
- Recovered Stage Requalification: Requalification can inspect hardware recovered by a qualified booster-return system or a surface lander. A returned physical article and its actual loading history remain required; knowing descent navigation is not proof of recovery.
- Reusable Launch System Integration: Powered, parachute or runway recovery can support a specified partially reusable architecture; all stages need not use the same method.
- Orbital Component Exchange: Compatible module exchange can be robotic or performed by qualified crew with actual tools and life support.
- Orbital Transfer Stage Reuse: A reusable carrier may use restartable chemical propulsion, electric propulsion, or exchanged finite solid-motor modules. A solid grain is not refilled or relit; actual replacement modules and compatible handling are required.
- Orbital Propellant Transfer: Compatible low-gravity feed can use surface-force acquisition or paid settling acceleration; neither route creates donor stock.
- Cryogenic Orbital Depot Integration: A depot may pay for active cooling or accept measured losses behind passive insulation; zero boiloff is not a universal prerequisite or guarantee.
- Deep-Space Clock Navigation: A characterized atomic or quartz reference supports one-way estimates only over its measured stability interval; calibration and bias estimation remain required.
- Asteroid Contact Processing: Processing depends on characterized feed. Magnetic separation or gravity concentration under qualified natural or imposed acceleration can be used; neither implies all asteroid material is valuable.

## Disadvantaged acquisition

Each new JSON record specifies five proposed recovery routes: paid scholar travel and instruction, reciprocal partnership, commissioned engineering, licensed/imported service and analysis of delivered hardware or records. Each route retains source competence, travel or transmission delay, finite local qualification and continuing operating inputs. A supplier cannot sell a proven result where it only has an unproven concept. A purchased flight transports only its contracted payload; it does not award the launch vehicle knowledge chain.

## Evidence and uncertainty

Recovery and refurbishment are separate activities in [NASA's reusable-booster maintenance lessons](https://llis.nasa.gov/lesson/836). The proposed alternative return graph uses this distinction without copying one historical vehicle architecture into a universal prerequisite chain.

Storage pressure, heat transfer and reduced or zero boiloff remain active engineering questions described by [NASA's Zero Boil-Off Tank program](https://www.nasa.gov/glenn/glenn-expertise-space-exploration/physical-sciences-program/fluid-science/zero-boil-off-tank-zbot/). The game designs therefore require measured heat-load closure and do not grant passive indefinite cryogenic storage.

The separation of refueling, repair and assembly follows the scope of [NASA's in-space servicing, assembly and manufacturing program](https://www.nasa.gov/isam/). This is support for mechanism distinctions, not proof that every proposed servicing mission or component is mature.

Beamed sails, momentum-exchange capture, sustained asteroid processing, large rotating interfaces and nuclear-electric mission architectures retain explicit conditional outcome records. Qualifying a principle does not promise useful scale, affordable throughput, successful arrival or a viable settlement. Full historical-horizon allocation and future coverage remain unfinished.
