# Military Equipment and Services Depth Review

This section adds 52 D20 proposals after removing eight repeated support or qualification applications. It deepens personal equipment, artillery transport, protected vehicles, mobile services, naval and air support, and bounded remote-platform operation. At this publication checkpoint the catalog accounts for **2,442 identities: 556 implemented baseline, 48 pending communications and 1,838 master drafts**. **2,558 remain to author.** Military systems accounts for 180 of its 280 editorial target, leaving 100.

Role labels below are proposed capability consumers, not newly implemented units or extra discoveries. The previously recorded 89 implemented military roles are unchanged by this document. These drafts add no troops, vehicles, stores, working platforms or player controls.

## General-led operation and distinct roles

Generals execute military operations in every era. The player communicates purpose and objectives through the existing conversation flow. Equipment discoveries change only supported capabilities once actual units possess compatible equipment, qualified personnel, supplies and maintenance. They do not make an order feasible, guarantee obedience or reveal unobserved enemy positions.

The proposed roles distinguish combat equipment from towing, recovery, mobile workshops, field power, replenishment, deck handling and aircraft servicing. Multiple discoveries can support the same role; a single service discovery can support several roles. Retiring a duplicate discovery therefore does not remove its proposed consumer role.

Eight proposals were removed: naval compartment damage isolation, warship propulsion redundancy, weapon condition inspection, armored-transport protection, military water treatment, medical field power, military spare forecasting and articulated-armor maintenance. Their relevant roles use existing damage-control, reserve, inspection, protection, water, electrical, forecasting or repair mechanisms. The reconciliation file records each retained role destination.

## Acquisition, readiness and physical operation

Every record specifies visiting instruction, reciprocal trials, commissioned research, licensed/imported supply and delivered-specimen examination. Sources must possess relevant knowledge or deliverable capacity. Real journeys, payments, local training and continuing support remain costs. Imported equipment does not manufacture its own ammunition or spare parts and does not teach its complete production process automatically.

A force can acquire compatible foreign transport or repair equipment and train local personnel before mastering domestic manufacture. It remains dependent on actual supplies, qualified repairs and supplier access. Retained equipment persists as equipment; an expired support agreement does not delete it, but unsupported failures, missing parts and exhausted stores can reduce its usable capability.

Carrier aircraft are not enabled by a ship label alone. Compatible deck space, recovery/launch systems, crews, aircraft maintenance, supplies and weather determine usable service. The general or authorized command staff decides employment. There is no player-operated launch minigame or mandatory deck-management form.

Mobile workshops only provide work after being transported and set up with real tools, utilities, parts and people. Recovery reaches actual known damaged equipment through feasible access; it does not conjure replacements or find unseen wrecks. Remote platforms consume energy, communication where needed, maintenance and accountable command authority. Observation reports retain source, time and uncertainty.

MilitaryCampaign retains soldiers, equipment and casualty pools. GovernmentPeopleSystem remains responsible for civic officials and daily settlement labor; HistoricalFigures retains exceptional contributors and independent field generals. No new population or service authority is introduced. Actual recruitment, paid manufacturing, readiness, field supply, role conversion, acquisition penalties, AI investment and general behavior remain to implement against these proposals.

## Grounding and limits

The accepted project design requires general-led battlefield decisions in every era and preserves actual personnel, equipment, supply and observed information. This section follows that contract rather than restoring direct cohort control. See `docs/GENERAL_CAMPAIGN_DESIGN.md`.

The Naval History and Heritage Command describes early carrier development as experimentation with deck and flight operations and records the varied uses of naval aviation. That supports separating ship/aircraft integration, deck work and supporting services rather than treating all naval aviation as one unconstrained capability. [NHHC aircraft carriers](https://www.history.navy.mil/browse-by-topic/ships/aircraft-carriers.html), [NHHC naval aviation](https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/nhhc/browse-by-topic/communities/naval-aviation.html).

These sources ground selected distinctions, not every proposed edge, historical first occurrence, equipment performance or role balance. The document intentionally describes game capabilities at a high level; detailed engineering recipes and operating tactics are not part of the review. Further semantic review and historical-horizon assignment remain necessary.
