# Records and Network Depth Review

59 additional D13 proposals after excluding a handwriting-recognition specialization already covered by general optical text recognition. These are authored designs, not completed network operations. Records and communications now account for 173 of 240 planned identities.

## Branching repairs

The earlier draft incorrectly placed digital schemas before general accessible records, digital replication before all archive replication, and space networking before long-delay correspondence. Those gates are removed. Tactile or spoken presentations, hand-copied archives and messages carried over long physical journeys remain available. Decentralized network coordination now has several routing alternatives rather than a universal consensus requirement.

Syllabic, consonantal and alphabetic writing are parallel conventions, not a mandatory ranked ladder. Tactile patterns require a readable code and real media. A new representation does not automatically teach the population to read it.

## Alternative route review

- Syllabic Script Encoding: Speech representation can develop on several existing media. These script routes are alternatives, not a ranked mandatory ladder.
- Consonantal Script Encoding: Speech representation can develop on several existing media. These script routes are alternatives, not a ranked mandatory ladder.
- Alphabetic Script Encoding: Speech representation can develop on several existing media. These script routes are alternatives, not a ranked mandatory ladder.
- Stable Document Locators: Stable section or item references can precede bound books; numbering needs a declared edition or record identity.
- Tactile Character Codes: Tactile symbolic conventions and trained reading can precede any digital schema. Pattern geometry, medium and actual reading skill remain necessary.
- Tactile Record Embossing: Relief-form or direct impression methods can produce tactile patterns before automated embossers; a liquid-writing nib is not a necessary prerequisite.
- Frequency-Division Multiplexing: Wired or radio signals may share a path through suitable frequency separation; no broadcast-only prerequisite is imposed.
- Time-Division Multiplexing: Mechanical or electronic timing and switching can support different qualified time-sharing systems; digital audio is not mandatory.
- Adaptive Channel Equalization: Qualified equalization can be analog or digital; automatic adaptation needs appropriate feedback and actual channel observations.
- Antenna Array Beamforming: Qualified measured electrical phase or delay control can develop alongside formal wave models; optical interferometer manufacture is not required.
- Optical Dispersion Compensation: Qualified compensation may be optical or performed in signal processing; measuring attenuation alone is not a necessary foundation for dispersion control.
- Packet Priority Scheduling: Shared queue disciplines can develop in earlier message switching, then specialize to packets; declared priorities do not create extra capacity.
- Network Admission Control: Admission decisions can use qualified capacity estimates or maintained circuit availability before modern packet reservation.
- Evidence Transfer Acceptance: Received evidence needs local interpretation whether carried physically or through a delayed digital link; delivery acknowledgment is not discovery mastery.

## Outside research and actual delivery

Every destination retains five proposed learning contracts: paid scholar instruction, partnership, commissioned work, imported or licensed services, and examination of delivered devices or authorized records. Knowledge transfer requires actual source access, negotiated contribution, travel or transmission, and finite local interpretation. A communication service can move evidence faster without awarding its contents as discoveries. A received file can be incomplete, unreadable or irrelevant; local evidence acceptance remains separate from delivery acknowledgment and mastery.

Links retain installed capacity, power, signal quality, routing state and reachable endpoints. Compression cannot remove every data cost; lossy coding explicitly loses information. A priority queue redistributes waiting time, and admission control can refuse work. Learning an archive-retention method never deletes records automatically.

## Evidence and limits

The distinction between maintained topology and calculated routes is illustrated by [RFC 2328's link-state protocol](https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc2328/). It supports the routing mechanism distinction, not a claim that the game implements OSPF or that every network needs the same protocol.

Equivalent encoded representations and language-sensitive ordering are separate operations, as specified in [Unicode normalization](https://unicode.org/reports/tr15/) and [Unicode collation](https://www.unicode.org/reports/tr10/). These sources do not establish the proposed historical script sequence or game learning costs.

Separate optical wavelengths can share a fiber through qualified channel arrangements; [ITU-T G.694.1](https://www.itu.int/t/aap/recdetails/9853) documents one such grid. The game does not require that exact real-world grid or assume unlimited channels.
