# Foreign production licenses

A production license permits operating a civilian manufacturing process without granting the associated discovery, adoption or research evidence. It is separate from buying a validated study. The current implementation covers processes represented by CivilianIndustry workshop recipes; military equipment and commissioned service plants are outside this first implementation.

The buyer needs Workshop Standards and Material Accounting. From an exposed manufacturing discovery, the existing foreign-research panel offers a one-year production license. The ordinary envoy quotation reserves real payment goods and round-trip food, and requires a known reachable foreign settlement. The quotation does not inspect hidden supplier knowledge. At arrival, the supplier must independently know the process at 35% adoption, retain at least one effective Crafting worker, have open sharing policy and be at peace with the buyer. It may refuse. The ordinary mission settlement credits accepted payment to the supplier on completion; refused payment returns once.

The contract activates only on return, lasts 365 simulation days and is recorded separately from known discoveries. It covers the civilian recipes gated by that process. Manufacturing still needs paid setup tooling, available workshop slots, ordinary craftspeople, material stocks and electricity where applicable. Licensed effective work is 65% of the corresponding independent line. Forecasts use the same reduction, and the production panel labels dependent operation. No tools, inputs, workers or output are granted by the contract.

At expiry, war, supplier withdrawal of open sharing, loss of the supplier or loss of its minimum skilled crafting capacity, dependent lines pause before consuming inputs. Existing goods, paid tooling and partial work remain. Restored support resumes an unexpired contract. Domestic knowledge at the normal 10% manufacturing-adoption threshold permits full independent operation even after the contract expires. Ordinary research retains its causal requirements; holding a license does not satisfy them. Ordinary encounter exchanges during an envoy journey remain separate from the license itself.

Renewal requires another paid physical mission and is offered in the last ninety days, or earlier when current supplier support is interrupted. Renewal replaces the one subject contract rather than stacking effects. The research inspector reports expiry and interruption; no automatic renewals or new notification flow are included yet.

## Persistence and limitations

An optional production_licenses dictionary in the existing society-exchange save stores subject, source, issued day and expiry. Validation limits the register to 5,000 records, accepts supported manufacturing subjects, and requires integer nonnegative days exactly 365 days apart. New license mission flags are accepted only in license mode. Older saves without the field remain valid. Older code does not implement these rights and rejects the new mission mode; backward operation in old builds is not supported.

This is a game contract model, not a legal patent system. Price negotiation, royalties, currency terms, automatic renewal, AI procurement strategy, licensed military manufacture, specialist staff assignments, commissioned service installations and recurring imported operating services remain unfinished. Supplier support is an aggregate policy/knowledge/crafting condition; the contract does not reserve an individual foreign expert's daily work. Detailed instruction packages, tacit knowledge and equipment-specific adaptation are abstracted by the throughput penalty and buyer prerequisites. The one-year term and 65% coefficient are explicit design values requiring campaign balancing.

Licensing opens manufacture; it does not promise access to every required material or operating service. Its interruption and independent-substitution behavior is tested in isolation, not yet in a centuries-long multi-civilization campaign. Canonical integration and native presentation remain outstanding.
