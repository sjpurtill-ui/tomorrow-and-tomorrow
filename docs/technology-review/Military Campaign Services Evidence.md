## Required implementation interpretations

The distinction between knowledge and equipment matters across this batch. Early access to a service idea cannot spawn its later industrial equipment. A field kitchen may use a qualified cooking method but still needs real vessels, fuel, labor and carriage. A decoy's material configuration determines its manufacturing chain and observable limits. A telephone service needs actual compatible equipment whose condition survives its operating environment.

Foreign help preserves these constraints. A paid teacher can transfer knowledge after arrival and instruction; a contracted service brings only staff and equipment that actually exist and arrive. Supplier dependence, travel, training and continuing support cannot disappear when the research label becomes known. Historical examples above do not independently validate the recovery system.

Billeting must not impose modern rights or budgets on every society. The simulation should use its actual rules for access, requisition and obligations, and expose effects on households. Salvage must retain inspectable identity and compatibility, with serviceable reissue distinct from repair. Strength returns must update the existing military roster interpretation, never create another population authority.

## Remaining historical review

Seven previously unsupported proposals now have named institutional examples. Survey datum control has technical support and related historical map evidence, but its separate discovery identity remains a semantic-review question. Early horizons for billets, baggage, communal field cooking, salvage, strength returns and decoys remain proposals until earlier regional examples are supplied. The World War I and II sources must not become universal invention dates. This evidence addendum therefore improves support without declaring historical review complete.
