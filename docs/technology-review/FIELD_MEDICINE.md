# Field medicine implementation review

The Medical Detachment is a support role, with zero offensive power, ordinary mobilized personnel, a 35-day authored base training requirement, and issued medical kits. Each kit reserves 1 Timber and 2 Fiber Plants and requires 2 workshop work units through the existing equipment system. These are game coefficients, not historical measurements.

All are causal prerequisites, not dates. Existing foreign study and partnership mechanisms can support locally valid learning routes. Methods do not create staff or equipment. The coefficients scale one aggregate care-capacity model; separate transfer paths, clinical priority queues and institutional case records are not yet simulated.

Effective staff is the lesser of people and issued kits, multiplied by training and personnel condition. Base capacity is 0.5 cases per effective person per recovery step. Adoption improvements sum, capped at +150%. Food-supply availability scales capacity. Each serviced case consumes 0.1 Fiber Plants and 0.05 Medicinal Plants and contributes 0.08 person to the existing fractional recovery accumulator. Stores and the recoverable wounded pool cap service. Raw resource categories currently abstract expendable care materials; no manufactured sterile dressings or pharmaceutical supply chain is claimed.

The supported locations are ordinary home-army preparation and the general-led campaign's home recovery/resupply stop. The existing systems account for the people, daily work, provisions, casualties and return to duty. No dead or permanently disabled people are restored, and medical formations contribute zero offensive combat power. The older broad Battlefield Medicine multiplier remains compatible.

Historical grounding distinguishes collection, transport, records and treatment rather than treating equipment alone as care. The [Army medical history of field operations](https://achh.army.mil/history/book-wwi-fieldoperations-chapter23/) discusses aid, collection and triage roles; the [medical-service prologue](https://achh.army.mil/history/book-wwii-medsvcsinmedtrnmnrthrtrs-prologue/) describes patient records accompanying the evacuation chain. These sources inform distinctions; they do not establish the game's prerequisite graph, dates, staffing ratios or numeric effects.

Outstanding work includes forward care deployment, real evacuation travel and capacity, replenishment outside home, disease and injury-specific outcomes, deployed supply packing, dedicated medical graphics and full campaign balancing. The research identities represent distinct practices, but their current operating consequences are deliberately aggregate. This checkpoint is not the complete military support system.
