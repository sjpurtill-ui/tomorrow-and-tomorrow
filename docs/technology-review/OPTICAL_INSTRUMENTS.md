# Optical instrument-making

Six authored methods add lens centering, eyepiece design, fine-focus stages, illumination apertures, specimen slide mounting and compound microscopy. They reconverge through existing lenses, controlled optics, precision machinery, bearings, glass and workshop standards. No calendar unlocks or discovery-granted instruments.

Six workshop recipes consume real glass, optical lenses, copper, metalwork and their manufactured intermediate assemblies. A microscope combines a centered objective, eyepiece, focus stage and illuminator. Slides are a separately manufactured supply. Components and instruments use the ordinary paid setup, shared Crafting labor, production stock, licensing and save systems.

A microscopy bench consumes one Compound Microscope and two Timber, then ten commissioning worker-days. An operating bench reserves half a Crafting worker and consumes 0.1 Specimen Slides per day for two units of specimen-observation assistance. It needs no electric illuminator in this initial model. Researchers still provide their normal Knowledge study work. Assistance adds at most 25% to that item's ordinary progress, cannot exceed its remaining work, and spends a finite shared daily budget across specimens. Paper support remains separately paid. The aid applies only to returned physical specimen records, not cultural accounts, purchased studies or partnership protocols. It changes examination speed, not evidence strength or automatic discovery ownership.

The component distinctions follow [Nikon's description of the microscope optical train](https://www.microscopyu.com/microscopy-basics/components): objective, eyepiece, specimen support and illumination optics have distinct functions. Recipe quantities, work rates and the observation bonus are game abstractions, not engineering specifications or claims of historical priority.
