# More rehearsed combined-arms practices

Three individually authored discoveries expand the existing formation-held doctrine system. Skirmisher–Infantry Screens pairs skirmishers with formed infantry against mounted threats. Engineer–Infantry Security pairs engineers with infantry against rifle, assault-infantry and machine-gun formations. Infantry–Antitank Coordination pairs infantry with antitank teams or tank destroyers against armored formations. All three have day zero and reconverge through existing weapons, drill, staff and specialist research.

Discovery permits rehearsal; it does not insert a defense bonus into society-wide statistics. The existing preparation system requires eligible target and support formations, adoption and rehearsal supply. Its stored practice grows gradually. In combat, supporting effective strength is reduced by equipment, ammunition, training, condition and readiness, while the matching threat share bounds the effect. Loss of supporting formations removes the benefit. The unchanged global doctrine-defense ceiling is 1.30. Nominal per-practice ceilings are 15%, 20% and 20%; these are game tuning values, not historical measurements. Generals continue to execute operations. There is no new tactical control screen or mandatory logistics form.

The broad emphasis on specialists operating with other arms is supported by the U.S. Army's discussion of [engineers operating within combined-arms formations](https://www.benning.army.mil/infantry/magazine/issues/2014/Jan-Mar/Smallfield.html) and its account of [mounted reconnaissance force composition](https://www.benning.army.mil/armor/earmor/content/issues/2012/SEP_OCT/Cameron.html). These sources support the combined-arms design principle, not the numerical modifiers or a universal historical sequence.

This remains aggregate combat coordination. It does not model screen geometry, observation distances, individual weapon lanes, breach success, mine clearance or an independent reconnaissance mission. The engineer practice protects the engineer formation; it does not grant automatic obstacle removal. No new unit archetypes or equipment are added. Existing formation doctrine dictionaries carry the new IDs; old saves remain compatible, and saves using new doctrines require this revision. Existing replacement dilution and round-trip save behavior remain in use.
