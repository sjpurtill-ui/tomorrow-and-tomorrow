# Technology chronology decision log

This file records settled direction. Reopen an item only when new evidence shows
that it produces a contradiction or unplayable result.

| ID | Decision | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C01 | The campaign reference arc lasts 2,500–3,000 years. | Ordinary successful play must tell a coherent story across that duration. |
| C02 | Eras are hidden editorial constructs. | No global era banners, era currencies, or date-based era unlocks appear to the player. |
| C03 | Acceleration and delay are legitimate. | Reference year bands measure narrative pacing; they do not prohibit exceptional progress. |
| C04 | Severe anachronisms are causal errors. | Advanced knowledge requires its indispensable material, energy, measurement, institutional, production, and logistical stack. |
| C05 | Historical change is progressive and uneven. | A civilization can be advanced in one field and weak in another; there is no single global era variable. |
| C06 | Discovery accelerates through positive feedback. | Records, surplus, specialization, institutions, instruments, networks, and computation increase research capacity for concrete reasons. |
| C07 | Advanced complexity resists an uncontrolled late-game waterfall. | Larger systems require more validation, capital, coordination, maintenance, and cross-field support. |
| C08 | Missing a favorable branch does not permanently remove a human capability. | Rebuilding, teaching, partnership, licensing, reverse engineering, and imported service remain possible with visible disadvantages. |
| C09 | Knowledge and operation are separate. | Knowing a method grants no free facility, material, equipment, labor, trained operator, or maintained service. |
| C10 | The early game has a deliberately slow formal-discovery rate. | Exploration, survival, relationships, projects, seasonal planning, exchange, conflict, and crises must carry play between rare breakthroughs. |
| C11 | Not every discovery interrupts play. | Major breakthroughs receive full announcements; supporting work goes to reports, program histories, and the archive. |
| C12 | The 5,000 target counts distinct mechanisms and capabilities. | Maturity labels, imports, translations, art variants, and alternate acquisition routes do not inflate the discovery count. |
| C13 | Planning precedes mass art and implementation. | Final art and runtime promotion wait until identity, scope, chronology, prerequisites, and presentation tier are accepted. |
| C14 | Tests follow implementation maturity. | Editorial planning uses document and ledger review; focused tests cover code changes; long simulations run only at pacing milestones. |
| C15 | The 5,000 identities are allocated across all 24 transformations and 24 fields. | New authoring fills a defined historical and disciplinary need; it does not grow an undifferentiated backlog. |

## Terms

**Reference chronology** is the expected progression of an ordinarily successful
civilization under ordinary investment. It is not a schedule imposed on every
campaign.

**Hidden era** is an editorial band used to judge narrative order and catalog
coverage. A civilization never owns an era.

**Transformation** is a progressive change in what a civilization can sustain,
such as durable records, industrial machine production, or networked computation.

**Anchor breakthrough** is a load-bearing discovery whose absence should prevent
or strongly constrain a major family of later capabilities.

**Severe anachronism** is a capability appearing without one or more indispensable
causal systems. Merely arriving earlier or later than the reference chronology is
not an anachronism.
