# The full 3,000-year, 5,000-discovery journey

This is the quantitative narrative plan for the complete technology game. It
connects the 24 hidden transformations to all 24 fields and reserves exactly
5,000 distinct discovery identities. It is an editorial authoring contract, not
a calendar gate, runtime unlock table, or claim that all reserved identities are
already implemented.

The player begins with a small community whose knowledge lives in people,
practice, landscape memory, and fragile traditions. The ordinary arc ends around
campaign year 3,000 with a civilization able to sustain planetary repair,
autonomous industry, durable settlements beyond familiar environments, and
projects whose consequences extend beyond a lifetime. A civilization can move
faster or slower. It cannot skip the physical and social foundations that make a
capability reproducible.

The overlapping dates describe diffusion and coexistence. They do not imply that

large because future capability is an integration problem spread across energy,
materials, medicine, institutions, computation, ecology, and space; it is not a
single speculative branch.

### T01–T04: survival becomes a place

**T01 — Ecological orientation.** The first strategic technologies are ways of
seeing: seasons, tracks, edible and dangerous species, water signs, routes,
weather cues, remembered places, and communicable signals. Discoveries emerge
from expeditions and repeated encounters. The player chooses what to observe,
which risks to revisit, and whose practical knowledge to trust.

**T02 — Controlled heat, tools, food, and shelter.** Fire becomes a maintained
physical practice. Cutting, boring, binding, coverings, hearth design, cooking,
fuel selection, and protected structures form mutually supporting branches.
Fire can be learned from another people, but embers, fuel, tenders, hearths, and
safe routines still have to exist locally.

**T03 — Containers, preservation, and planned reserves.** Basketry, skins,
cordage, ceramic containers, drying, smoking, salting, store design, carrying,
counting, spoilage recognition, and reserve customs turn immediate survival into
seasonal planning. Surplus creates new tensions: theft, allocation, obligation,
guarding, rot, pests, and unequal control.

**T04 — Durable settlement and managed subsistence.** Cultivation and animal
management split into locally valid branches rather than forming a universal
farming unlock. Water access, seed continuity, breeding, waste separation,
building repair, tenure, defense, childbirth, care, and seasonal labor make a
settlement persist. Failure can drive migration without erasing learned methods.

### T05–T08: settlements become regions

**T05 — Managed water, sanitation, and construction.** Wells, drains, cisterns,
street surfaces, foundations, fired materials, waste removal, inspection, and
maintenance let population exceed the easy capacity of a site. Infrastructure
creates permanent service obligations and public-health consequences.

**T06 — Specialized crafts, measures, and markets.** Workshops, apprenticeship,
gauges, standard measures, contracts, quality comparison, exchange media, market
records, specialist provisioning, and repair separate expert production from
household improvisation. The player gains better goods while accepting dependence
on skill, supply, and political protection.

**T07 — Traction, wheeled transport, and regional navigation.** Pack and draft
animals, harness, wheels, axles, roads, bridges, boats, sails, route surveys,
freight packaging, depots, and repair services extend effective geography.
Terrain and water routes keep different transport branches valuable.

**T08 — Regional authority and organized warfare.** Revenue, stores, offices,
levies, command hierarchy, drill, arms production, fortifications, reconnaissance,
field care, supply groups, and negotiated service turn violence into a sustained
institution. Generals conduct operations; the player sets objectives, appoints
people, funds preparation, and responds to consequences.

### T09–T12: memory becomes institutional

**T09 — Durable writing, accounting, and archives.** Scripts, numeracy, media,
ink, copying, indexing, sealed correspondence, maps, archives, and custody let
instructions and obligations survive distance and death. Literacy remains a
trained capacity with material costs, not a civilization-wide switch.

**T10 — Engineered materials and monumental systems.** Controlled kilns and
furnaces, ore preparation, alloy families, glass, binders, geometry, lifting,
structural design, ship structures, inspection, and planned repair support works
larger than a household. Monumental failure is expensive and politically visible.

**T11 — Learned institutions, law, and administration.** Schools, libraries,
scholarly lineages, courts, hospitals, offices, credentials, curricula, formal
mathematics, and comparative records preserve difficult knowledge. Scholar envoys
become available here: payment, hospitality, status, translation, and political
trust buy instruction, while the recipient still builds the operating stack.

**T12 — Long-distance commercial and military networks.** Ports, ocean
navigation, credit, insurance, cargo standards, diplomatic practice, postal
relays, intelligence, professional forces, siege systems, repair, and medical
support allow action far from home. Network disruption propagates through prices,
readiness, food, and legitimacy.

### T13–T16: craft becomes industry

**T13 — Mechanical power and reproducible machinery.** Water and wind power,
rotary transmission, gears, bearings, pumps, lathes, gauges, machine elements,
workshop standards, and maintenance organizations create machines that help make
better machines. Site, season, wear, and skilled fitting remain binding limits.

**T14 — Print, instruments, and empirical inquiry.** Paper supply, printing,
publishing, broader literacy, correspondence, optical and physical instruments,
experimental controls, replication, statistics, scientific societies, and
technical education let criticism accumulate. Knowledge spreads faster, but bad
methods and political suppression can also spread farther.

**T15 — Thermal, chemical, and metallurgical process systems.** Thermometry,
pressure models, coke, furnaces, acids, alkalis, reactors, vessels, analytical
comparison, fertilizers, medicines, explosives, and waste controls bind research
to operated plants. Scale-up becomes a separate problem from laboratory success.

**T16 — Steam transport, factories, and mass administration.** Reliable engines,
machine tools, interchangeable production, factories, rail, telegraph, industrial
finance, labor organization, statistics, urban services, artillery, and national
logistics create compounding throughput. Pollution, labor conflict, accidents,
capital concentration, and supply shocks are part of the transformation.

### T17–T20: industry becomes a network

**T17 — Electrified cities, public health, and precision industry.** Generation,
grids, motors, lighting, electrical measurement, clean-water services,
refrigeration, precision production, hospitals, and safety regulation form
interdependent urban systems. Reliability and load balancing matter as much as
nominal generation.

**T18 — Motor logistics, flight, broadcast, and combined arms.** Refined fuels,
engines, road vehicles, aerodynamic measurement, aircraft, radio, weather
services, airfields, broadcast institutions, mechanized supply, armor, artillery,
air defense, and coordinated commands compress distance. Fuel, maintenance,
training, detection, and civilian exposure govern useful strength.

**T19 — Electronic sensing, computation, and automatic control.** Electronic
components, radar and other sensors, formal logic, memory, programming, feedback
control, digital communications, fabrication, testing, and software operations
make data an operating dependency. Automation can increase output and magnify a
bad model, failed sensor, or insecure instruction.

**T20 — Global networks, molecular science, and planetary observation.** Large
computers, interoperable networks, satellites, molecular measurement, controlled
clinical systems, high-purity production, supply-chain coordination, and global
models permit many simultaneous programs. Purchased research and licenses are
common here, but operating capacity, validation rights, updates, and supplier
dependence remain separate costs.

### T21–T24: networks become long-lived civilization

**T21 — Resilient planetary systems and ecological repair.** Dense observation,
climate and ecosystem models, verified restoration, carbon and nutrient accounts,
circular production, reserve design, disaster recovery, and transregional
governance turn environmental action into measured long projects. Intervention
creates delayed, uncertain, and politically distributed consequences.

**T22 — Autonomous high-energy and high-precision industry.** Trusted autonomy,
resilient computing, advanced generation, robotic production, closed-loop
metrology, extreme materials, provenance, assurance, remote repair, and recovery
permit sustained work in dangerous or remote environments. Human oversight and
graceful failure remain technologies and institutions in their own right.

**T23 — Durable oceanic, orbital, and offworld settlements.** High-capacity
transport, pressure and radiation protection, local resource processing, closed
life support, habitat construction, medicine, demography, governance, rescue,
and maintenance autonomy turn missions into settlements. A settlement can be
politically independent while remaining materially dependent.

**T24 — Multigenerational human futures.** Long-duration health and reproduction,
distributed archives, skill custody, intergenerational law, planetary defense,
remote autonomous science, durable habitats, cultural continuity, and
century-scale project institutions determine what survives. The endpoint is a
set of supported choices, not a compulsory singularity or guaranteed utopia.

## Branching and recovery contract

Every discovery has common operating foundations and one or more ways to learn
it. The catalog expresses these separately:

- `requires_all` holds the foundations every route must respect;
- `requires_any` holds genuine alternative causal paths;
- a learning route states how evidence or instruction is obtained; and
- operating requirements state what must exist to use and maintain the result.

The major learning routes are:

1. **Local practice:** slow accumulation through repeated work and observation.
2. **Directed inquiry:** people, materials, instruments, and time assigned to a
   question.
3. **Specialist tradition:** apprenticeship or an institution preserves tacit
   practice.
4. **Specimen study:** imported artifacts or observed processes narrow the search
   without teaching every production step.
5. **Scholar envoy:** from late H02 and especially H03, a visiting expert teaches
   for payment, trade, protection, patronage, or reciprocal access.
6. **Partnership:** two societies share people, evidence, facilities, and risk;
   coordination is slower but can bridge missing local expertise.
7. **License or purchased research:** chiefly H04 onward and routine by H05;
   purchase speeds adoption while imposing cost, scope limits, dependence, and
   weaker independent redesign.
8. **Reverse engineering:** a physical example and adequate measurement shorten
   discovery, with higher material waste and reliability risk.
9. **Reconstruction:** archives, surviving practitioners, ruins, and retained
   equipment rebuild a lost operating tradition.

Leaning on another civilization is therefore a viable disadvantaged strategy.
It costs time, bargaining power, access, dependence, or fidelity. It never
creates a furnace, grid, hospital, trained crew, supply chain, or command system
by implication. No favorable branch is permanently mandatory: an isolated
civilization can build an endogenous route, but usually with more experiments,
failures, and opportunity cost.

## Discovery acceleration without a waterfall

The curve accelerates because the civilization changes how it learns:

| Horizon | Dominant feedback | Simultaneous work | Primary brake |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H01 | Memory, imitation, repeated seasons | Very few inquiries | Subsistence, travel, loss of practitioners |
| H02 | Surplus, workshops, apprenticeship, exchange | Several local inquiries | Local evidence, transport, patronage |
| H03 | Records, schools, archives, scholar travel | Multiple institutional programs | Literacy, copying, political continuity |
| H04 | Print, instruments, societies, industrial demand | Specialized programs | Capital, energy, precision, replication |
| H05 | Universities, firms, states, global communication | Large portfolios | Validation, regulation, infrastructure |
| H06 | Automation, simulation, planetary networks | Very large portfolios | Coupled risk, rare inputs, assurance, long trials |

Early discoveries can take decades of game time while seasons, expeditions,
relationships, construction, scarcity, and conflict provide the decisions.
Late civilizations can complete many supporting discoveries in parallel, but
anchors require broader stacks and longer verification. The growing catalog does
not mean 1,300 modal interruptions in H06; most late work appears inside programs
and digests.

## Military journey

D20 reserves 280 primary military discoveries: 35 in H01, 55 in H02, 55 in H03,
45 in H04, 45 in H05, and 45 in H06. Military capability also consumes transport,
medicine, materials, energy, records, computation, governance, and manufacturing.
Those supporting discoveries keep their own primary fields and are not counted a
second time.

The military arc progresses through hunting and watch practices; weapons and
local defense; formations, command, stores, fortification, scouting, siege, and
field care; professional forces and long-distance supply; firearms, artillery,
staff work, rail and industrial mobilization; motorized combined arms, aviation,
signals, detection, and electronic command; then resilient autonomous support,
orbital awareness, remote operations, verification, and planetary defense.

A discovery can authorize a weapon, doctrine, service, training course, or unit
design. It does not conjure an equipped unit. Recruitment, personnel, training,
equipment production, transport, maintenance, ammunition, medicine, command, and
supply determine fielded capability. Generals continue to execute battlefield
operations in every horizon; the player shapes objectives, appointments,
readiness, force structure, diplomacy, and acceptable risk.

## What counts among the 5,000

A counted identity must introduce a distinct mechanism or capability and have a
testable consequence. The following do not create extra identities by themselves:

- historical renamings or regional art variants;
- merely knowing, adopting, scaling, or mastering the same method;
- imported, licensed, translated, or independently rediscovered copies;
- arbitrary quality levels without a new mechanism;
- one technology repeated for each unit or building that consumes it; or
- a broad umbrella whose components already carry all gameplay consequences.

Discovery, local adoption, installed capacity, reliable operation, and mature
scale remain different state. Their separation supplies much of the gameplay
without inflating the catalog.

## Authoring and implementation order

The 5,000 slots are now reserved, but identities enter the game only through
bounded transformation packets:

1. Reconcile existing live and authored identities to the allocation matrix.
2. Fill and repair the transformation's anchor spine.
3. Select 20–50 connected supporting identities around one playable change.
4. Define common stacks, alternate learning routes, operating requirements, AI
   use, save ownership, and presentation tier.
5. Implement the wave and run focused subsystem checks once.
6. Review the resulting play before authoring the next connected wave.

At the end of each hidden horizon, audit shortest causal paths, branch recovery,
interruption density, operating consequences, and reference pacing. Long campaign
runs begin only after several complete transformations can produce meaningful
evidence.

## Allocation authority

`journey-allocation.json` is the machine-readable authority for field, horizon,
transformation, role, and presentation totals.
`transformation-field-allocation.tsv` is the reviewable 24-by-24 matrix.
`build_journey_allocation.py` deterministically regenerates both and rejects any
total other than 5,000. These files remain planning data and must never become
calendar-based runtime prerequisites.
