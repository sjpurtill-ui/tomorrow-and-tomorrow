# Hidden chronology: 24 progressive transformations

The campaign clock begins at year 0 and normally reaches its future horizon
between years 2,500 and 3,000. These bands are fictional campaign references,
not a compression of one specific Earth chronology. Bands overlap because
different systems develop at different rates and because a transformation takes
generations to diffuse.

Nothing in this document is a calendar unlock. A transformation is present only
when its required capabilities operate at meaningful scale.

## H01 — Survival and early practice

Reference band: campaign years 0–350. Research is informal, local, fragile, and
embedded in ordinary work. Breakthroughs are rare and memorable.

| ID | Reference window | Transformation | Required world | Change experienced in play |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| T01 | 0–80 | Ecological orientation | Repeated observation, remembered routes, seasonal movement, shared signals, and practical classification of local hazards and foods. | Expeditions become purposeful; the community can return to known places and anticipate recurring conditions. |
| T02 | 40–160 | Controlled heat, food preparation, and protected shelter | Maintained fire, fuel collection, cutting and binding tools, workable coverings, safe hearth practice, and teachable routines. | Cold, darkness, cooked food, material transformation, injury risk, and camp permanence become strategic concerns. |
| T03 | 100–260 | Containers, preservation, and planned reserves | Fiber and clay working, drying or smoking, storage places, counting or remembered obligations, and labor beyond immediate consumption. | The community prepares for lean seasons, supports longer journeys, trades surplus, and risks theft or spoilage. |
| T04 | 200–380 | Durable settlement and managed subsistence | Reliable water, repeated cultivation or managed herds, construction knowledge, waste separation, seed or breeding continuity, and defendable tenure. | Place becomes politically meaningful; households invest in land, buildings, stores, and future harvests. |

### H01 research feedback

Knowledge grows through memory, imitation, repeated seasonal evidence, respected
practitioners, and contact. There are few simultaneous inquiries. Loss of a skilled
person or community can materially delay a method without causing artificial
forgetting of widely practiced knowledge.

## H02 — Settlement and regional exchange

Reference band: campaign years 250–900. Surplus and permanent places support
specialists, regular exchange, local offices, and organized defense.

| ID | Reference window | Transformation | Required world | Change experienced in play |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| T05 | 280–480 | Managed water, sanitation, and durable construction | Surveyed water, drainage, wells or conveyance, fired or selected materials, maintained streets and structures, and assigned communal labor. | Settlement health and capacity depend on maintained infrastructure rather than location alone. |
| T06 | 380–620 | Specialized crafts, measures, and regular markets | Repeatable measures, apprenticeship, workshops, accounting traditions, transport containers, enforceable exchange, and dependable surplus. | Equipment quality and supply chains differentiate communities; specialists require protection and provisioning. |
| T07 | 480–760 | Traction, wheeled transport, and regional navigation | Managed animals or equivalent motive work, roads or prepared routes, wheel and axle production, boatbuilding, wayfinding, maintenance, and freight organization. | Resources, armies, officials, and information operate across a region rather than only near the settlement. |
| T08 | 600–900 | Regional authority and organized warfare | Stores, levies or taxation, command hierarchy, formation training, weapons production, logistics, public legitimacy, and negotiated obligations. | War and government become sustained institutions; conquest creates administration and supply problems rather than a single battle reward. |

### H02 research feedback

Specialists preserve deeper craft traditions. Markets and travel carry examples
between communities. Administrative continuity and apprenticeships increase the
number of inquiries, but evidence remains local and replication is slow.

## H03 — Complex societies and learned traditions

Reference band: campaign years 700–1,550. Durable records, formal learning, law,
engineering, finance, and long-distance networks preserve knowledge beyond one
community or generation.

| ID | Reference window | Transformation | Required world | Change experienced in play |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| T09 | 750–1,050 | Durable writing, accounting, and archives | Stable notation, writing media, trained readers and scribes, copying, custody, indexing practices, and institutions that preserve records. | Obligations, designs, law, history, and distant instructions survive people and journeys with visible costs and distortion risks. |
| T10 | 850–1,200 | Engineered materials and monumental systems | Controlled furnaces and kilns, ore or mineral supply, measured geometry, lifting and transport, specialized crews, inspection, and repair. | Fortifications, civic works, ships, weapons, and production facilities acquire long service lives and costly failure modes. |
| T11 | 1,000–1,350 | Learned institutions, codified law, and public administration | Schools or scholarly lineages, archives, courts, offices, revenue, standardized procedures, and protected time for study. | Expertise becomes an institution that can advise, resist, fail, or outlive a ruler; policy can be compared across generations. |
| T12 | 1,150–1,550 | Long-distance commercial and military networks | Navigational knowledge, credit and contracts, standardized cargo, ports and roads, professional forces, medical support, repair, intelligence, and diplomacy. | The civilization can project power and dependency far from home, while disruptions propagate through linked regions. |

### H03 research feedback

Records and learned institutions permit cumulative work. Translation, commentary,
scholar missions, conquest, and trade broaden the evidence base. Research is still
limited by travel time, patronage, literacy, instruments, and the continuity of
institutions.

## H04 — Mechanization and industrial systems

Reference band: campaign years 1,300–2,150. Mechanical power, printing,
measurement, chemical process control, precision tools, and concentrated capital
begin a sustained acceleration.

| ID | Reference window | Transformation | Required world | Change experienced in play |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| T13 | 1,300–1,650 | Mechanical power and reproducible machinery | Rotary power, gearing and drives, bearings, standardized parts, machine-supported measurement, workshops, skilled maintenance, and concentrated work. | Production capacity depends on installed machines, energy sites, spare parts, and trained operators. |
| T14 | 1,450–1,750 | Printing, broad technical records, and organized empirical inquiry | Cheap repeatable records, publication and distribution, mathematical notation, instrument making, correspondence, schools, and reproducible demonstrations. | Knowledge travels farther and can be criticized or reproduced; states and institutions contest control of information. |
| T15 | 1,600–1,950 | Thermal, chemical, and metallurgical process systems | Quantified heat and pressure, controlled feedstocks, furnaces and vessels, pumps, analytical comparison, waste handling, and capital-intensive plants. | New materials, fuels, medicines, explosives, and pollution bind research directly to industrial operation. |
| T16 | 1,750–2,150 | Steam transport, machine production, and mass administration | Reliable engines, coal or equivalent energy, precision machine tools, rails or high-capacity routes, factories, finance, statistics, standards, and national logistics. | Production and movement accelerate sharply; urbanization, labor politics, industrial war, and systemic shortages appear. |

### H04 research feedback

Printed knowledge, standardized instruments, industrial demand, professional
societies, and specialized laboratories create reinforcing growth. Capital and
energy become major constraints. Independent discovery, licensed production, and
industrial espionage diverge meaningfully.

## H05 — Modern scientific and networked systems

Reference band: campaign years 1,950–2,700. Electrification, precision science,
public health, motor transport, electronics, computation, molecular biology, and
global networks allow many simultaneous research programs.

| ID | Reference window | Transformation | Required world | Change experienced in play |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| T17 | 1,950–2,250 | Electrified cities, public health, and precision industry | Generation and grids, electrical measurement, motors, lighting, clean-water services, laboratories, interchangeable production, trained professions, and regulatory capacity. | Night work, communications, hospitals, factories, and essential services form an interdependent urban system. |
| T18 | 2,050–2,350 | Motor logistics, powered flight, broadcast communication, and industrialized combined arms | Refined fuels, engines, light alloys, aerodynamics, radio, road and airfield systems, maintenance records, mass supply, and coordinated commands. | Distance contracts while fuel, spare parts, weather, detection, civilian exposure, and logistics dominate strategy. |
| T19 | 2,200–2,500 | Electronic sensing, computation, and automatic control | Semiconductor or electronic components, reliable power, precision fabrication, formal logic, memory, software practices, sensors, feedback control, and communications networks. | Administration, industry, science, intelligence, and warfare become dependent on data and complex technical infrastructure. |
| T20 | 2,350–2,700 | Global networks, molecular bioscience, and planetary observation | Large-scale computation, interoperable networks, satellites or equivalent sensing, molecular measurement, controlled trials, high-purity production, and international technical institutions. | The civilization can observe and coordinate at planetary scale while facing systemic cyber, ecological, biological, and supply-chain risks. |

### H05 research feedback

Research becomes a large social sector supported by universities, firms, states,
automated instruments, global communication, and purchased data. Growth is rapid,
but expensive validation, regulation, specialization, and infrastructure make
advanced programs dependent on broad systems.

## H06 — Advanced planetary and human futures

Reference band: campaign years 2,500–3,000 and optional continuation. These
transformations require integrated performance beyond ordinary modern practice.
Modern methods used in a future setting remain H05 unless their authored scope
adds a genuinely new capability.

| ID | Reference window | Transformation | Required world | Change experienced in play |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| T21 | 2,500–2,750 | Resilient planetary systems and ecological repair | Dense observation, verified intervention models, transregional governance, circular material systems, reserve capacity, long-term monitoring, and enforceable responsibility. | Prosperity depends on maintaining planetary systems; repair choices create delayed benefits, uncertainty, and international obligations. |
| T22 | 2,600–2,850 | Autonomous high-energy and high-precision industry | Trusted automation, resilient computation, advanced energy, closed-loop metrology, extreme materials, self-diagnosing plants, secure supply provenance, and human oversight. | Production can operate in hostile or remote environments, but failures become highly coupled and assurance becomes a central institution. |
| T23 | 2,700–2,950 | Durable oceanic, orbital, and offworld settlements | Launch and transport capacity, life support, radiation and pressure protection, local resource processing, maintenance autonomy, medicine, governance, reproduction research, and reliable rescue or refuge. | Settlements become societies with demographic, political, ecological, and dependency choices rather than temporary missions. |
| T24 | 2,850–3,000+ | Multigenerational human futures and long-baseline civilization | Proven long-duration habitats, distributed archives and skills, intergenerational law, advanced health support, planetary defense, remote autonomous science, and institutions able to sustain century-scale projects. | The campaign turns toward what humanity chooses to preserve, alter, distribute, and become; outcomes remain contingent rather than guaranteed. |

### H06 research feedback

Automation, simulation, planetary networks, and accumulated knowledge support very
large research programs. New capabilities still face extreme validation burdens,
long experiments, rare materials, coupled risks, and political coordination.
“Future” is not a universal speed multiplier.

## Chronology review rule

A discovery may sit earlier or later than its common historical analogue when its
causal stack supports that placement. Reviewers should reject it when any
indispensable stack is absent, especially:

- usable energy and its distribution;
- appropriate materials and feedstock quality;
- manufacturing precision and repeatability;
- instruments, measurement, evidence, and validation;
- trained people and knowledge preservation;
- organization, finance, regulation, or command;
- transport, communications, supply, and maintenance; or
- a genuine operating context in which the capability can be reproduced.

Reference chronology is therefore a diagnostic baseline. The causal world remains
the authority inside the game.
