# T05 managed water, sanitation, and construction review

This packet fills the ten T05 anchor slots before another runtime wave. It reviews the canonical 878-discovery catalog against the T05 reference window, campaign years 280–480. The dates remain narrative references rather than calendar locks.

The two retained operating anchors already act through paid local assets. The other eight have credible identities and causal knowledge, but grant some transformation credit before the required structure, water, material, or maintenance exists.

## Filled slots

1. **Controlled Kilns** — repeatable fired construction material and later process heat.
2. **Latrine Siting** — separated waste ground and a new maintenance obligation.
3. **Protected Wellheads** — physical protection of an existing well rather than new supply.
4. **Rainwater Cisterns** — finite seasonal storage with distinct lining routes.
5. **Water Settling Basins** — bounded clarification without a sterilization claim.
6. **Gravity Conduit Grade Control** — surveyed, paid, condition-dependent water movement.
7. **Shallow-Foundation Assessment** — site evidence applied to selected building work.
8. **Lime Mortar** — manufactured binder feeding installed and repairable masonry.
9. **Graded Roads** — drained, surfaced, traffic-bearing routes with recurring repair.
10. **Irrigation Schedules** — recorded water turns tied to actual delivery and fields.

## Accepted causal order

T05 grows from T04 rather than replacing it. Settled labor, drainage, water-site knowledge, framed construction, stores, measures, and recurring administration support several branches:

- pit firing and tempered clay lead to controlled kilns;
- drainage and clean-water practice expose waste separation;
- well siting plus drainage lead to protected wells;
- drainage plus one valid lining tradition lead to cisterns;
- clay shaping plus a well or cistern route leads to settling basins;
- survey and drainage support gravity conveyance;
- site assays and load trials support foundation assessment;
- kiln heat, limestone and shared measures lead to lime mortar;
- remembered routes, drainage and recurring labor support graded roads; and
- crop calendars, drainage and tallies support irrigation scheduling.

These branches can interleave. A dry-region civilization can emphasize cisterns and irrigation, a wet settlement can prioritize drains and raised routes, and a stone-poor settlement can retain timber and earthen construction while improving water and sanitation. No single material route is the era gate.

## Chronology exceptions requiring repair

### Infrastructure knowledge still acts as infrastructure

Kilns, latrines, wellheads, cisterns, settling basins, mortar, roads, and irrigation currently grant at least part of their global effect from knowledge and adoption. T05 requires operating scale: paid assets, suitable locations, staff, finite inputs, condition, and maintenance. The discovery remains necessary, while local operation controls the effect.

### Water quantity, water quality, and waste are too loosely separated

A cistern can increase stored quantity without making water safe. A settling basin can remove suspended material without sterilizing it. A protected wellhead can reduce surface contamination without increasing aquifer flow. Latrines can improve sanitation only when their siting and condition keep waste away from water and occupied ground. The runtime wave must keep these four claims separate.

### Roads and irrigation lack bounded service networks

Graded Roads currently contribute broad movement and haul effects, and Irrigation Schedules contribute cultivation yield without a finite delivery network. Both need local length or field coverage, real construction or water allocation, condition, and recurring labor. Knowledge should expose projects and operating rules rather than repaint every route or field.

### Controlled kilns bridge too many later families without capital

Controlled Kilns support ceramics, lime, glass, assays, and later refractory work. An adopted idea cannot provide simultaneous unlimited firing capacity. The first implementation should establish a bounded kiln service before later T05 and T10 families draw on it.

## Supporting discoveries reviewed with implementation

The first runtime packet should inspect, but does not promote to additional T05 anchors: Clay Pipe Forming, Ceramic Pipe Firing Qualification, Ceramic Pipe Fit Gauges, Clay Pipe Socket Jointing, Rigid Pipe Bedding, Conduit Infiltration Testing, Adobe-Wall Construction, Wattle-and-Daub Walls, Thatched Roofing, Lime Burning, Soil Infiltration Trials, Foundation-Settlement Monitoring, Irrigation Loss Accounts, and the existing building and water maintenance services.

## Proposed implementation order

1. **Water and waste works:** physical latrine, protected wellhead, cistern, and settling-basin projects with quantity, quality, siting, staff, condition, and cleaning separated.
2. **Kiln and binder service:** bounded firing capacity, lime burning, mortar production, and existing construction consumers.
3. **Route and irrigation networks:** paid graded route coverage and finite water turns tied to actual fields.
4. **T05 transformation evaluation:** compute progress from operating coverage across water, sanitation, durable fabric, roads, and assigned communal labor. Do not expose the hidden transformation label in ordinary play.

Each implementation packet gets focused tests for changed owners and one adjacent integration run. No long campaign simulation is required until the complete T05 operating slice exists.
