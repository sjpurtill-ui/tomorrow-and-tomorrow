# The 300-slot anchor breakthrough spine

The full catalog can contain 5,000 discoveries without making all 5,000 equally
important. The anchor spine is the causal skeleton that carries the civilization
through the 24 hidden transformations. Supporting discoveries deepen, diversify,
or provide alternate routes through that skeleton.

This document allocates 300 anchor-review slots. A slot is an editorial budget,
not a promise to invent another identity. Existing discoveries should fill slots
where their scope is suitable. Several closely coupled identities may be reviewed
together, but each retained discovery remains distinct.

## Selection test

A discovery belongs on the spine when removing it would make a major later family
physically, institutionally, or intellectually implausible. At least one of these
must be true:

1. It creates a new source or scale of usable energy.
2. It creates a new class of material, machine, instrument, or production system.
3. It changes how information can be preserved, verified, transmitted, or computed.
4. It creates an institution needed to sustain specialized work across generations.
5. It changes the practical scale of settlement, transport, health, government, or war.
6. It integrates several mature systems into a capability that none provides alone.

Popularity, historical fame, visual appeal, and a strong bonus are insufficient by
themselves.

## Required cross-field stacks

Every H04–H06 anchor must declare which indispensable stacks it consumes. A stack
may be satisfied by several causal routes, but it cannot be omitted.

| Stack | Questions the anchor must answer |
| --- | --- |
| Energy | What supplies usable power, fuel, heat, or motive work? How is it delivered? |
| Materials | Which feedstocks and material qualities are required? Who produces them? |
| Manufacture | Which tools, tolerances, controlled environments, and repeatability are necessary? |
| Measurement | How is performance observed, compared, calibrated, and validated? |
| Information | How are instructions recorded, transmitted, interpreted, and revised? |
| People | Which trained roles perform, supervise, teach, and repair the work? |
| Institution | Which organization funds, coordinates, authorizes, or regulates operation? |
| Logistics | How do inputs, components, people, waste, and replacement parts move? |
| Maintenance | What degrades, what detects failure, and what restores service? |

## Branching rule

An alternate learning route may replace a way of learning an anchor. It may not
replace the anchor's common operating foundations.

For example, foreign instruction may supply compressor design evidence. Reliable
refrigerated freight still requires power, insulation, freight equipment,
manufacturing, refrigerant handling, trained operators, and maintenance. A license
may permit bounded production without granting independent redesign knowledge.

## Spine review sequence

1. Fill T01–T04 from the live catalog and identify missing opening anchors.
2. Fill later transformations with existing live identities before using drafts.
3. Mark every anchor `keep`, `repair`, `merge/split review`, or `missing`.
4. Inspect the shortest causal route to every H04–H06 anchor.
5. Repair bypasses at the shared-stack level rather than adding arbitrary chains.
6. Select supporting discoveries only after the transformation's anchors are sound.

The first implementation wave begins only after T01–T04 anchors and their
presentation tiers have been reviewed together with the early-game experience.
