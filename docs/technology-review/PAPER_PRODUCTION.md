## Consumable benefit

The existing 15% Knowledge-worker effort used to examine returned collections continues without paper. When Paper is stocked, each supported work unit consumes 0.01 batch and yields 20% more examination progress. The calculation caps consumption by available paper, actual worker effort and the unfinished record's remaining work. Unused effort can examine the next record. Completed records, future arrivals and repeated processing of the same day consume nothing.

This applies to examination of returned accounts, specimens and partnership protocols through the existing collection loop. It does not grant a discovery, waive its local prerequisites or increase the strength of its eventual evidence. Paper can be used without knowing its manufacturing process, so imported supplies remain useful. The collection panel explains the consumption and the no-paper alternative.

## Historical grounding and limits

The Library of Congress describes plant-fiber rags as a papermaking feedstock and beating with stampers as a way to break them into slurry. Its preservation guidance also distinguishes paper-fiber quality and sizing. These support separating preparation from sheet manufacture; they do not establish the game's numerical research bonus. [Papermaking: A Rags to Riches Story](https://blogs.loc.gov/bibliomania/2024/12/05/papermaking-a-rags-to-riches-story/), [Paper deterioration and preservation](https://wwws.loc.gov/preservation/care/deterioratebrochure.html).

The electric beater is explicitly a powered implementation, not a claim that the earliest mechanized paper mills used electricity. The current cloth stock represents plant textiles; wool and synthetic compatibility are not modeled. Cloth pulping consumes usable cloth, not an invented waste stream. No automatic waste recovery, continuous paper machine, bleaching, sizing, ink, paper grades, drying climate, effluent, archival deterioration or printing consumption is implemented here. Existing broad papermaking/printing effects remain and need further physical-system reconciliation.
