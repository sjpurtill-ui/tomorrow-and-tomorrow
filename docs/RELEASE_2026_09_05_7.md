## City force rules

Previously, siege entry required only an arrived nonempty army; winning a battle
changed city ownership without sufficient occupation survivors; destructive
orders checked an occupation record rather than effective control.

The existing population/resistance occupation requirement is now enforced for
city capture and control. Effective personnel account for supply and readiness.
New garrisons detach enough actual soldiers to meet effective requirements where
available, conserving the source army. Unsupported occupations retain existing
people/history but do not generate occupation production/market/food benefits.
The UI distinguishes presence from effective control and gives reinforcement or
withdrawal routes. Peaceful reform and return of control remain available.

Siege entry and daily progression enforce perimeter coverage. The existing
sqrt(population)*5 + defenders*.3 + relief perimeter model now includes a
fortification multiplier; a sustained investment requires half that coverage in
supplied troops. Insufficient forces cannot impose blockade/pressure. Existing
undersized sieges lift on their next daily evaluation. This is a tunable aggregate
balance model, not a historical staffing claim or a universal minimum army size.

Coercive administration requires the ordinary garrison plus resistance-scaled
city-wide enforcement capacity. Resident operations cannot use more personnel
than remain after garrison duties, and commit the force for 30 days. Razing,
coercive policies, resident killing and coerced transfers share the authoritative
checks, including revalidation at execution. Previously saved occupations are
preserved; insufficient force no longer confers effective destructive authority.
Voluntary citizen transfers retain their existing logistical rules.
