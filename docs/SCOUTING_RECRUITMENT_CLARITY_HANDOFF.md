## Player behavior

Scouting is a standing 0–10% population allocation with Exploration & discovery or Recruitment & influence. The toolbar shows the allocation and people away. Staff choose affordable departures, retain seven civilian food days, respect available adults and party capacity, pay for actual expedition provisions and replace returning parties. Turning the policy off stops new departures; people already away still travel home. GovernmentPeopleSystem remains the owner of civilian labor and deducts the existing mission-absence ledger. Opponent controllers issue the same policy command according to their ruler's existing priorities.

Staff routes use a bounded search over connected ground, favoring places outside returned charts and other departing parties' routes. Checked side visits and alternate return approaches form a continuous physical circuit when terrain and provisions allow. A narrow coast may require retracing. No policy or route preview reveals fog. Actual returned paths remain the source of charted ground. Short local routes no longer keep a party away or charge provisions for a full requested year. Targeted reconnaissance remains available from a city report, showing outward travel, observing time and return separately.

City reports fit a compact floating sheet with flag, civilization, dated population estimate, six small metric cards and expandable provenance. Escape or a map click dismisses it. Map labels and reports show the same original observed range. A separate, explicitly unverified planning projection accounts for age; the old formula that inflated a small town from 46–70 to approximately 5–139 after a year is removed. Repeated days physically near a city improve estimation. Travel days do not count as observation, and older records without observation-duration evidence do not receive fabricated precision.

Recruitment fills missing people in groups that fit free training places, recruitable people, available equipment and extra rations. It keeps existing soldiers and their experience at home. A target of 50 with 12 already home needs 38 recruits, not a new class of 50. If 29 places are free, 29 can enter now and the remaining nine follow automatically. Equipment stocks are shown once per weapon type, with issued and reserved equipment separate. The first page shows At home / Training / To recruit, immediate intake, the limiting resource and a single standing order. Stopping recruitment stops future groups. Research, prototypes, food reserves and training-policy gates still apply.

## Performance

The old command-rail toolbar called a terrain route planner during refresh even when no scout departure was ordered. Instrumentation of the copied day-11238 campaign measured interface updates around 0.84–1.46 seconds in affected frames. Removing that quote from both toolbar paths reduced the measured interface update to about 3–9 milliseconds. This is a measured component improvement, not a whole-game speed guarantee.

The longer 80-day run found a separate monthly primary-settlement refresh spike: one measured footprint rebuild took 2.84 seconds. It rebuilt close vegetation even at a regional camera distance where those nodes are hidden. Detailed vegetation now waits until its visible distance and uses the actual plot/road clearance fields to distinguish physical changes from upkeep-only revisions. The LOD transition checks pending changes before showing it. Twenty-three landscape/seasonal/secondary-city cases pass, including new hidden-distance and physical-clearance invalidation cases.

A shared troop catalog calculates owner positions once per observer refresh and still gives each observer an independent mutable copy. A sparse exact chart index avoids repeatedly scanning every old trail for route novelty. Staff planning uses at most two connected searches of 96 nodes rather than multiple distant A* searches. It retains land checks, knowledge restrictions and the same daily civilization rules. No opponent, population, resource turn or research turn is skipped.

Detailed post-change headless timing of the copied actual campaign, with 12 opponents, measured 0.25–0.34 seconds on ordinary days and 0.37 seconds on the initial day. The final normal-rendering release replay advances 85.029 days in 30.020 seconds (2.832 days/second) at the 3-day setting, with 246 rendered frames. Before the hidden-vegetation fix, the same release probe measured 1.828 days/second and 70 frames. These are short local measurements under varying host load, not a guaranteed speedup factor or stable 3-day rate. The experimental separate render thread was tested but is not enabled. Integration evidence is recorded in INTEGRATION_STATUS.md. Synchronous daily simulation remains a frame-time limitation; do not claim smooth graphics merely because a throughput benchmark improves.
