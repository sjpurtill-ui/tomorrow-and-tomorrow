# Historical military roster selection

Target: **130 field unit archetypes across all 18 eras**, selected from the study's 398 catalog entries, plus **10 command and strategic capabilities**. This is the expanded design roster. The current 11 combat types and twenty-seven animated prototypes cover only a small initial slice.

Source: `C:/Users/sjpur/TomorrowandTomorrow/docs/Historical_Military_Unit_Progression.docx`. The document supplies research and candidate units; these selections and production priorities are game-design decisions. The original study is unchanged.

## Selection rules

Favor distinct battlefield jobs, readable silhouettes, meaningful counters, and manageable asset reuse. Include naval, aviation, reconnaissance, and support forces. Regional alternatives such as camel cavalry, mounted retainer archers, war junks, and macuahuitl warriors provide different development paths. They are not universal upgrades.

An era below is a featured production grouping, not the unit's invention date. Older units persist. Unlocks should depend on people, materials, institutions, training, command, sustainment, and information; a calendar date alone should not grant a unit. Exact source horizons and enablers are preserved in the adjacent JSON.

## Command and strategic capabilities

Keep these in the broader military system, represented by headquarters, formations, facilities, or operational overlays as appropriate. They do not each require an infantry model. Their inclusion here does not implement nuclear, cyber, or satellite mechanics.

- **Field headquarters**: Plan marches, camps, supply, and battle.
- **General staff**: Coordinate large forces, maps, logistics, mobilization.
- **Ready reserve brigade**: Trained formation held below full active strength.
- **Ballistic-missile brigade**: Theater or strategic long-range strike.
- **Nuclear stewardship unit**: Secure, maintain, and account for nuclear stockpiles.
- **Satellite reconnaissance program**: Observe broad areas and strategic targets.
- **Military communications satellites**: Extend command and data globally.
- **Cyber defense command**: Protect networks, identities, software, data.
- **Cyber operations group**: Authorized network reconnaissance and effects.
- **Theater missile-defense unit**: Intercept some ballistic or cruise threats.

## Next modeling batches

1. **Early ranged and mobility — visual prototypes built:** slingers, javelin skirmishers, chariot archers, horse archers, crossbow infantry, camel cavalry. These fill major gaps in the first eras with distinct weapons and movement.
2. **Classical formations and siege — visual prototypes built:** pike phalanx, legionary heavy infantry, war elephants, battering ram, siege tower, trireme.
3. **Medieval and early gunpowder — visual prototypes built:** armored foot soldiers, pavise crossbowmen, long-range bow corps, counterweight trebuchet, hand-cannon team, bombard.
4. **Pike through flintlock:** pike-and-shot formation, arquebusiers, musketeers, pistol cavalry, bayonet infantry, grenadiers.

Use the existing spearman, bowman, lancer, ballista, cannon, rifleman, machine gun, and truck as reusable starting points where suitable. Adapt equipment, armor, posture, and proportions for the selected archetype. An existing generic model is not proof that a historical variant is finished.

## Modeling and large-scale battles

Share skeletons and animation families where possible while retaining recognizable weapons, shields, vehicles, and silhouettes. Each combat model needs idle, movement, attack, and death/disabled clips; attacks should reflect its weapon rather than reuse a generic swing. Support units need role-appropriate work and disabled states.

Keep army strength numeric. Draw capped representative formations near the camera, with cheaper representations at distance. Naval and aircraft units need their own movement, spacing, and animation handling; they cannot simply be added to the infantry crowd shader. A pike-and-shot formation is a mixed formation recipe, not one new soldier mesh.

## Implementation boundary and coverage

This selection does not change recruitment, balance, unlocks, equipment stores, or the running combat simulator. The runtime still has 11 broad unit types and twenty-seven animated prototype assets. Expanding runtime types needs explicit definitions for capabilities, costs, counters, equipment, and formations as each batch is integrated.

The JSON records every selected unit's exact study name, source table and row, historical horizon, purpose, constraints, family, and candidate existing asset. Source table numbers are one-based across all document tables; data rows exclude each table header. All selected names have been checked against the study, with no duplicate unit IDs.
