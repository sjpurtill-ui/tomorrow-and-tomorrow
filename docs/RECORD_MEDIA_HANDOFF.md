# Physical record media for local study

Worktree `/Users/seanpurtill/.codex/worktrees/ef8f/tomorrow-and-tomorrow`, branch `codex/physical-record-media`, base `0826ab216c5b6227f3d18c2f7133f4b1454e23f1`. Scope: three record recipes and knowledge definitions, finite study supplies, the single SocietyExchange study consumer and study-specific civilian supply planning. No rail, shipment, government labor or save-schema changes.

Three existing identities now produce actual supplies: prepared clay tablets from clay and water, quantity-record cords from plant fiber, and bound paper volumes from paper, yarn and covers. Paid tooling and completed civilian work are required. Partial jobs retain actual paid progress and finish once through the game's binary save representation. Record media are allocated to study notes using the existing Knowledge workforce; buying a blank medium neither teaches manufacture nor completes a discovery.

Printed sheets, bound volumes, paper, clay tablets and eligible cords share one work budget. Supply assistance cannot overshoot the remaining study or consume materials when no work remains. Ordinary study continues without media. Clay and bound-volume supplies support comparison of arrived local collections. The implemented knotted-record use is restricted to specimen quantity observations and requires locally adopted interpretation; it is not a general manuscript or translation format. Existing paper/print behavior and arrival/day guards are preserved.

The ordinary civilian planner can produce record supplies when paper is unavailable, follows actual upstream materials and respects its ten-unit stock target. Bookbinding requires cordage and actual paper inputs; manufacturing the paper is not a mandatory learning prerequisite. The prior authored paper/parchment prerequisite is retained by reconciliation. Parchment manufacture and a general archive-storage simulation remain unimplemented.

Sources support the record mechanisms: [Metropolitan Museum clay letter](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/325849) and [Museo Larco quipus](https://www.museolarco.org/en/exhibition/permanent-exhibition/online-exhibition/textiles-from-ancient-peru/inca-quipus/). The bounded quantity-context rule is this game's implementation scope, not a claim about all historical uses of knotted records. Material quantities and study assistance are game tuning.
