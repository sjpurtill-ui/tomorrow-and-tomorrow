# Great works that grow with society

New commissions carry a frozen `budget` on the existing undertaking record.
The budget is a share of construction capacity, not a fixed ancient material bill.
Modest, grand and audacious works commit 20% of reference construction output for
2, 6 and 12 years respectively. Each engineering tier adds 20% to that horizon.
Reference capacity includes current productivity and a minimum construction base
of 2.5% of the settlement population, so starving the construction allocation
cannot purchase a cheap national monument. With fewer builders, construction takes
longer. Pressing the crews uses the existing 50% allocation and hardship rules.
The original form's work remains a floor for small settlements.

Costs are charged incrementally by the existing construction ledger. Advanced
finishes also consume Civilian Goods. Later growth never reprices an ongoing work.
Assessment, decisions, progress, expenditure and completion read the same budget.
Existing commissions without a budget retain their previous work and rewards.
Renovation layers preserve and restore their budgets and earned rewards.

Six knowledge-based engineering tiers govern new designs. Later proposals favor
engineered public infrastructure over early rings and mounds. New higher-tier
works have larger dimensions and richer complexes; industrial designs add glazed
roofs, visible structural ribs and entrance galleries. Existing historical works
keep their recorded appearance. Geometry is batched, with bounded details.

New rewards gain 20% strength per tier. Storage is scaled to the population at
commission, capped at 200,000 units after craftsmanship payoff; percentage bonuses
retain the existing aggregate caps. New famine reserves scale to population,
with a 100,000-ration cap, and still contain only food actually sealed from surplus.
Influence and allure gain tier strength while retaining the existing global caps.
