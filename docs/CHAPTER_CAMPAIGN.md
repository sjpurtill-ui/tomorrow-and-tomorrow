# Continuous civilization, independent chapter advancement

Approved direction, November 6, 2026: ten chapters covering the existing 0–3000
research progression in 300-year content windows. They are historical scopes,
not synchronized calendar transitions. People, knowledge, production, armies,
territory, relations and consequences persist. A civilization can advance before
its neighbours; the same actor-scoped rules apply to every shared-simulation actor.

## Play loop

Research opens with the current chapter's three breakthrough routes and actual
capacity comparisons. Each route has two named catalog discoveries. Any one route
plus 90 sustained qualifying days completes the chapter. The requirements are:

- Military: at least eight, or 3% of population, whichever is larger, in existing
  formations with training at least 25%, personnel condition at least 60%, and
  at least 80% of required equipment. Food security must be at least 80%.
- Productive: actual civilian goods cover the society's current goods target,
  at least one effective maker works, and food security is at least 80%.
- Civic: effective administrators cover 2% of population (minimum one), while
  legitimacy and cohesion each reach 65%.

These are gameplay thresholds, not historical claims. No troops, goods, food,
research capacity or modifier are granted by the chapter ledger. Newly qualifying
conditions earn one observed day; subsequent continuous simulation steps can
credit their span. A shortfall resets that route; duplicate days and unobserved
calendar jumps cannot manufacture a sustained run. Existing coarse opponent
steps retain their interval approximation; this does not reconstruct daily
conditions inside a skipped interval.

The completed record retains the route, date, population, discoveries and exact
capacity snapshot. Completion advances automatically, at most once per simulation
step. The tenth completion is recorded once and ordinary play continues.

## Research

The chapter tree displays actual prerequisite connections for its selected field,
with immediate cross-field/earlier foundations shown distinctly. Connections
include alternative routes; the selected question's existing detail explains the
exact ALL/ANY requirements. Chapter and field buttons are direct choices. The
graph pans and zooms and retains its viewport across live refreshes. It supports
all existing catalog nodes in the chapter; it is not a cosmetic linear milestone
track. The full knowledge archive remains available.

Question selection uses the existing research-target API. Field +/- controls
change attention shares within the existing finite research workforce. Explicit
route focus funds previously unfunded prerequisite fields with one attention
share and makes free teams prefer eligible route prerequisites in their field.
Existing manually pinned questions remain authoritative. Finishing a question
frees its team; focus does not create researchers or discount the actual work.
Clearing the route focus retains the current shares and returns free teams to
quickest-question scheduling. Advisers use the same scheduler and actor rules.

Earned chapter access removes the ahead-of-date work surcharge through that
chapter's upper year. All prerequisite, evidence, resource, staffing, production
and adoption requirements remain. The existing calendar and learning lead can
still support research further ahead, at the existing extra cost when applicable;
advancement does not hard-lock older research or forbid exceptional early inquiry.
Chapter completion itself never follows the calendar. It does not jump the world
clock, change demographic ages, grant a civilization-wide combat bonus, or reset
learning goods and maintenance costs.

## First-chapter military pressure

The old 300-year immunity to unprovoked pressure is replaced with two founding
years, followed by an eight-year ramp. Full ordinary pressure therefore starts
at year ten. This applies to player/rival and rival/rival encounters. The existing
seeded hazard, reachable routes, contact, treaties, relative strength, weariness,
generals, paid manpower and supply rules remain authoritative. There is no spawned
chapter army and no compulsory war objective. Later chapters do not reset the
respite. Fighting, deterrence and negotiated peace remain viable responses.

## Persistence and limits

DiscoverySystem owns the small reflected chapter ledger. Its graph/focus caches
are transient objects. Older saves without the ledger enter at the chapter of
their most advanced known discovery, explicitly marked as an existing campaign;
no earlier results are fabricated. Saves that already have chapter data preserve
it, including partial runs and focus. Legacy opponents only receive shared rules
when the campaign actually uses shared opponent simulation; the implementation
does not silently convert a legacy world.

This implements the chapter loop and uses the existing technology and military
mechanics in all ten chapters. It does not add multiplayer, new technologies,
scripted chapter invasions, or chapter-specific campaign maps. The thresholds,
90-day sustain period and first-decade pressure require sustained play balancing;
fixture tests and UI captures do not establish that all ten chapters are equally
challenging or fun. The chapter scopes follow the game's existing compressed
historical timeline, not literal AD dates.
